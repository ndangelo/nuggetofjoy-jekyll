<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="toc-wvpg-what-is-a-vector-image"><strong>1.&nbsp;</strong>What Is a Vector Image?</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FUiRufl7vUw&amp;t=60s">Watch video lesson (1 min) ↗</a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Adobe Illustrator is famous for creating eye-catching vector illustrations. Now if you’re wondering what a vector illustration actually is, we can basically describe it as<strong>&nbsp;a type of computer graphic that is made up of points, shapes, curves, and lines, instead of pixels.</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/illustrator_01-4.jpg" alt="vector vs pixels"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>One of the cool things about vector images is that they can be scaled up or down without losing any quality. So if you have a small vector image of a circle, you can make it bigger without it becoming blurry or pixelated.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Because of this, vector images are commonly used in things like logos, icons, and illustrations, because they can be resized and edited easily, without losing quality. They are also smaller in file size compared to other types of images, making them more convenient for storage and sharing.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="toc-dc19-how-to-import-a-sketch-image"><strong>2.&nbsp;</strong>How to Import a Sketch Image</h2>

<p>So now that we know what a vector image is,&nbsp;let’s&nbsp;go ahead and get started in Illustrator. If you ever change your mind about the dimensions of your document, you can change it later on by using the&nbsp;<strong>Artboard Tool.</strong></p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="toc-klr4-how-to-use-shapes-pathfinder-tools"><strong>8.&nbsp;</strong>How to Use Shapes &amp; Pathfinder Tools</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FUiRufl7vUw&amp;t=840s">Watch video lesson (5 mins) ↗</a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Select the&nbsp;<strong>Rectangle Tool (M)</strong>&nbsp;from the side toolbar to create shapes for the books and bookshelf.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/illustrator_24.jpg" alt="rectangle tool "/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Once you’ve got all the shapes ready, we can use the&nbsp;<strong>Direct Selection Tool</strong>&nbsp;to click and drag the&nbsp;<strong>live corner widgets</strong>&nbsp;to round the corners.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/illustrator_25.jpg" alt="round the corners"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Now open up the <strong>Pathfinder </strong>panel. This tool has different modes allowing us to combine and manipulate shapes. These will be detailed below:</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Unite: </strong>This unites your selected shapes. The resulting shape keeps the color attributes of the shape in front.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Minus Front:&nbsp;</strong>This allows you to subtract the frontmost selected shape or path from the shape or path behind it. It effectively cuts out the front shape from the back shape.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Intersect:</strong> This operation creates a new shape that intersects the selected shapes or paths. Only the overlapping areas are retained.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Exclude:</strong>&nbsp;This operation removes the overlapping areas of the selected shapes or paths, creating a new shape with the remaining non-overlapping parts.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Divide: </strong>This operation divides the selected shapes or paths into sections based on their intersections. It cuts the shapes or paths at the intersecting points, creating separate pieces.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Trim:&nbsp;</strong>This operation trims the parts of shapes or paths that extend beyond the overlapping areas, leaving only the shared areas.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Merge:&nbsp;</strong>This operation combines the selected shapes or paths into a single shape, preserving all the individual shapes' outlines.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/illustrator_26.jpg" alt="pathfinder panel"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>As you can see, the Pathfinder panel is extremely useful, so experiment with the different modes to get a feel for it and discover what is useful for you when creating your illustrations.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/illustrator_27.jpg" alt="unite tool"/></figure>
