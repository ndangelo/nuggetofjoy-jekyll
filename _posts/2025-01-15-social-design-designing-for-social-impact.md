<h1 class="wp-block-heading" id="9d32">What is Social Design</h1>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"fontSize":"medium"} -->
<p class="has-medium-font-size" id="b9c0">Social design is an application of design methodologies that solves complex human issues by deeply understanding people's experiences with these problems. It tackles issues of sustainability, health, technology, and vulnerability. It uses the human-centered design mindset as a creative launchpad in solving these complex issues.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<h1 class="wp-block-heading" id="7a99"><strong>Core Principles of Social Design</strong></h1>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="9a3c">1. Ethics and Empathy</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image {"linkDestination":"custom","align":"center"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><img src="https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:700/1*Hy6EQHdIzZlgVqC5Mu2MaA.png" alt=""/><figcaption class="wp-element-caption">Grammarly :&nbsp;<a href="https://www.grammarly.com/blog/empathetic/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">https://www.grammarly.com/blog/empathetic/</a></figcaption></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"fontSize":"medium"} -->
<p class="has-medium-font-size" id="cc14">There is a significant difference between sympathy and empathy. Sympathy involves feeling sorry for someone, while empathy is about putting yourself in their shoes. In fact, being empathetic is a crucial soft skill for any designer. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"fontSize":"medium"} -->
<p class="has-medium-font-size" id="c687">Ethics is about understanding your role in society and acting responsibly and morally towards the people you’re designing for.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="47d6">2. Social Literacy</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image {"linkDestination":"custom","align":"center"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><img src="https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:640/1*kF8dE6TJT1FdWZ2uIT15pg.jpeg" alt=""/><figcaption class="wp-element-caption">Photo by&nbsp;<a href="https://unsplash.com/@joshcala?utm_source=unsplash&amp;utm_medium=referral&amp;utm_content=creditCopyText" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Josh Calabrese</a>&nbsp;on&nbsp;<a href="https://unsplash.com/s/photos/empathy?utm_source=unsplash&amp;utm_medium=referral&amp;utm_content=creditCopyText" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Unsplash</a></figcaption></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"fontSize":"medium"} -->
<p class="has-medium-font-size" id="6dc8">Social designers need to grasp the context, complexity, and interconnectedness of social problems. They should also cultivate positive human values and cultural sensitivity to create effective interventions.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="919c">3. Design Research</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image {"linkDestination":"custom","align":"center"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><img src="https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:640/1*_6m5oan5gXdaWEaYEExVOQ.jpeg" alt=""/><figcaption class="wp-element-caption">Photo by&nbsp;<a href="https://unsplash.com/@mariopurisic?utm_source=unsplash&amp;utm_medium=referral&amp;utm_content=creditCopyText" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Mario Purisic</a>&nbsp;on&nbsp;<a href="https://unsplash.com/s/photos/people?utm_source=unsplash&amp;utm_medium=referral&amp;utm_content=creditCopyText" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Unsplash</a></figcaption></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"fontSize":"medium"} -->
<p class="has-medium-font-size" id="ccce">Involve stakeholders in the research process to identify the problem and create a solution. The research process is typically divided into two main types: Qualitative Research and Quantitative Research. These methods are used to collect, interpret, and apply data in different ways, often through ethnographic approaches.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="5a4a">5. Design Thinking + System Thinking</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image {"linkDestination":"custom","align":"center"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><img src="https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:700/1*J6sMkQ4kYnVVx9K7uWdvZQ.png" alt=""/><figcaption class="wp-element-caption">Design Thinking by&nbsp;<a href="https://www.cdhn.org/design-thinking" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Community Development and Health Networ</a>k</figcaption></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"fontSize":"medium"} -->
<p class="has-medium-font-size" id="e64a"><strong>Design thinking</strong> is a creative problem-solving approach that involves key stages: Understanding, empathizing, defining, prototyping, testing, and refining the solution collaboratively to effectively address the social problem. D-school design thinking, Ideo’s design thinking, and the Double diamond design thinking model are some of the widely popular tools used for solving social issues.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"linkDestination":"custom","align":"center"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><img src="https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:700/1*0ZLmtsvWt7xZ4fqpP4UcjA.png" alt=""/><figcaption class="wp-element-caption">Donnella Meadows on&nbsp;<a href="https://in.pinterest.com/pin/523191681708938659/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Pinterest</a></figcaption></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"fontSize":"medium"} -->
<p class="has-medium-font-size" id="775b"><strong>System thinking</strong>&nbsp;is a strategical problem-solving approach to help to solve problems that are dynamic and complex in nature.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"fontSize":"medium"} -->
<p class="has-medium-font-size" id="0f1d">For example, eradicating the hunger problem would be complex and dynamic in nature due to various factors involved in the problem in a country, such as poor living conditions, employment status, etc., and hence, it needs system-level thinking to solve it.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"linkDestination":"custom","align":"center"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><img src="https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:588/1*_QJDNa3V4u-EJnGE9y0hcw.jpeg" alt=""/><figcaption class="wp-element-caption">Conjointly using systems thinking and design thinking (<a href="https://idilgaziulusoy.com/2012/06/11/system-innovation-for-sustainability-using-systems-thinking-and-design-thinking/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Coughlan and Ponto, 2012)</a></figcaption></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"fontSize":"medium"} -->
<p class="has-medium-font-size" id="c9d9">Based on the complexity and nature of the problem statement, system thinking and design thinking can be conducted independently or co-dependently.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
