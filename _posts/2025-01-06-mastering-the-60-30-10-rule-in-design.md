<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In design, having a good color balance is important for creating visuals that look great and work well. One effective way to do this is by using the&nbsp;<strong>60-30-10 rule</strong>. This simple guideline helps you choose colors that work together nicely. Let’s explore what this rule is, how to use it, and why it’s helpful.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Understanding the 60-30-10 Rule</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The&nbsp;<strong>60-30-10 rule</strong>&nbsp;splits your colors into three parts:</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>60% Dominant Color:</strong> This is your main color. It should cover most of your design. This color sets the mood and gives a strong foundation for your project.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>30% Secondary Color:</strong> This color adds depth and supports the main color. It can be used for sections, backgrounds, or buttons to create interest without being too much.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>10% Accent Color:</strong> This color is a highlight that draws attention. It’s great for important buttons or messages that need to stand out.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">How to Apply the 60-30-10 Rule</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:list {"ordered":true} -->
<ol class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Choose Your Colors:</strong> Start by picking a main color that fits your brand. Use tools like Adobe Color or Coolors to find colors that go well together.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Create a Color Palette:</strong> Once you have your main color, pick a secondary and an accent color. Make sure they match nicely. For example, if your main color is blue, a light gray can be a good secondary color, with a bright orange as the accent.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Use the Colors in Your Design:</strong> Apply the main color to the background or large areas. Use the secondary color for sections that need some difference. Save the accent color for buttons and important highlights.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Test and Adjust:</strong> After applying the colors, see how users react. Gather feedback to check if the color balance works well. Make changes if needed to improve the experience.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ol>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Why the 60-30-10 Rule Matters</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The 60-30-10 rule helps not just with looks, but also with function:</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Visual Order:</strong> By using this color structure, you can guide the viewer’s eyes. This makes it easier for them to find their way around your design.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Brand Identity:</strong> Using consistent colors helps people recognize your brand. The 60-30-10 rule supports this consistency while allowing room for creativity.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Better User Experience:</strong> Good colors can create feelings and influence choices. A balanced color palette leads to a more enjoyable experience, encouraging users to engage with your content.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->
