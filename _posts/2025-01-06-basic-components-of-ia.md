<p>Dive into the basic components of information architecture and understand its purpose in UX design</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Have you ever been utterly lost on a website or app? Good information architecture (IA) prevents this and does more. IA is about organizing and structuring <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/content" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">content</a> so users can easily find what they need. It's like a map guiding you through the site.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In this lesson, you will learn the full extent of the role IA plays in&nbsp;<a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/user-experience" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">user experience</a>. You will explore its basic components, such as organizing content, labeling systems, and&nbsp;<a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/navigation" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">navigation</a>. By the end, you will understand how good IA makes websites and apps more user-friendly and efficient.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">What's information architecture?</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/whats-information-architecture-1721048740058/a-1721048740059.jpg" alt="What's information architecture?"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/whats-information-architecture-1721048740058/a-1721048740059-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Information architecture is about organizing and structuring information in a way that makes it easy for people to find and use. Imagine you have a library full of books. Without a system, finding a specific book would be hard. IA is the system that arranges the books into categories, <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/labels" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">labels</a> them, and provides a map of where to find each one.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In digital products, IA involves deciding how content is grouped, what terms are used, and how users navigate through the information. Good IA helps users quickly find what they need, making their experience smooth and pleasant.SaveShare</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Context</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/context-1721048778531/a-1721048778531.jpg" alt="Context"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/context-1721048778531/a-1721048778531-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Information architecture has 3 main pillars: context, content, and users.<sup><a href="https://app.uxcel.com/#anchor-1">[1]</a></sup> Let's break them down one by one. Context is about understanding the environment in which the information exists. It includes the goals, mission, constraints, and the devices on which the information will be accessed. For example, think of a music streaming app. The context includes providing a vast library of songs, supporting various genres, offering features like playlists and recommendations, and ensuring compatibility with devices like smartphones, <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/tablets" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">tablets</a>, and computers. This context shapes how the music is organized and presented.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>By understanding the context, we can ensure that the app meets its goal of delivering an enjoyable and user-friendly music experience. This makes the information more relevant and useful for its intended audience.SaveShare</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Content</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/content-1721048822426/b-1721048822430.jpg" alt="Content Bad Practice"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/content-1721048822426/b-1721048822430-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/content-1721048822426/a-1721048822427.jpg" alt="Content Best Practice"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/content-1721048822426/a-1721048822427-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Content is the actual information that needs to be organized. In the case of a music streaming app, content would include songs, albums, playlists, artist profiles, and music videos. It's important to make sure that this content is organized in a way that makes sense. For example, songs should be grouped by genre, albums, and artists. Playlists can be created for different moods or activities.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Each piece of content should be easy to find and clearly labeled. This helps users quickly find the music they want, whether they're <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/search" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">searching</a> for a specific song or exploring new genres.SaveShare</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Users</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/users-1721048933871/a-1721048933871.jpg" alt="Users"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/users-1721048933871/a-1721048933871-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Users are the people who will use the product, and it's important to understand the needs and preferences of different users to build effective IA. For example, in a music streaming app, some users might want to discover new music, while others might prefer listening to their favorite songs. By considering different user preferences, the app can provide features like creating custom playlists, sharing songs with friends, and providing music suggestions based on listening history.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/user-research" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">User research</a> is the best method to truly understand the needs and preferences of users. By conducting <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/interview" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">interviews</a>, surveys, <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/usability-testing" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">usability tests</a>, and other more in-depth forms of <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/research-knowledge" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">research</a>, you can gain insights into how users interact with your <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/user-interface" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">UI</a> and what features they find most valuable. This helps make your product more enjoyable and accessible.SaveShare</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Organization systems</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/organization-systems-1721048987508/b-1721048987510.jpg" alt="Organization systems Bad Practice"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/organization-systems-1721048987508/b-1721048987510-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/organization-systems-1721048987508/a-1721048987508.jpg" alt="Organization systems Best Practice"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/organization-systems-1721048987508/a-1721048987508-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Organization systems in IA are methods used to arrange and categorize information. They help users find what they need quickly. These systems include different ways of grouping, labeling, and ordering information. For example, an online library could organize books alphabetically, by genre, author, or based on the date of publishing. Good organization systems make websites and apps user-friendly, ensuring people can locate information without frustration.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Choosing the type of organization system depends on key user tasks. For example, if most users often search for books by author, organizing the books alphabetically by author’s last name might be best. User research activities like <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/card-sorting" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">card sorting</a> and creating <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/personas" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">personas</a> can help you understand user behavior and identify top user tasks.SaveShare</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Labeling systems</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/labeling-systems-1721049032121/b-1721049032132.jpg" alt="Labeling systems Bad Practice"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/labeling-systems-1721049032121/b-1721049032132-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/labeling-systems-1721049032121/a-1721049032121.jpg" alt="Labeling systems Best Practice"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/labeling-systems-1721049032121/a-1721049032121-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Labeling systems help users understand and find information quickly. These systems include various methods like:</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Categorization:</strong> Grouping items under clear labels like "Electronics" or "Furniture" on a retail website without using jargon.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Contextual links:</strong> This involves using labels embedded within the content to guide users to related topics.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Navigational terms:</strong> These are used in <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/menus" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">menus</a> and <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/buttons" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">buttons</a> to help users move around a site, such as "Home," "About us," or "Contact."</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Keywords:</strong> These are specific terms users might search for, helping in indexing and search functions.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Taxonomy:</strong> This is a structured way to organize information to reflect how users think and behave. It ensures that similar items are grouped together logically.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>To enhance user understanding of your site or app, ensure you use clear and meaningful labels consistently throughout the interface.SaveShare</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Navigation systems</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/navigation-systems-1721049075906/b-1721049075909.jpg" alt="Navigation systems Bad Practice"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/navigation-systems-1721049075906/b-1721049075909-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/navigation-systems-1721049075906/a-1721049075906.jpg" alt="Navigation systems Best Practice"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/navigation-systems-1721049075906/a-1721049075906-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Navigation systems help users find information within interfaces. They guide users through content and features. Here are some examples:</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Global navigation</strong>: This is usually at the top of a website. It has <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/links" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">links</a> to the main sections like "Home," "About," "Services," and "Contact."</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Footer navigation</strong>: Found at the bottom of the <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/pages" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">page</a>, it often repeats the main links and adds extras like "Privacy Policy" or "Terms of Service."</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Sidebar navigation</strong>: A side menu with links to related content or subcategories. It helps users explore a section deeply.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Breadcrumbs</strong>: Shows the path taken to get to the current page, like "Home > Products > Electronics > Cameras." It helps users understand where they are and how to go back.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>A good navigation system is clear, concise, and helps users find what they need quickly.SaveShare</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Search systems</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/search-systems-1721049109615/a-1721049109615.jpg" alt="Search systems"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/search-systems-1721049109615/a-1721049109615-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Search systems help users find specific information quickly on websites or apps. They allow users to type in keywords or phrases to locate content without browsing through menus. For example, if someone is looking for "<a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/ux-design" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">UX design</a> tips," they can type it into the search bar and get a list of relevant articles or pages.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The importance of search systems lies in their ability to save time and improve user experience. They make it easier for users to find what they need, especially on large sites with lots of content. Good search systems provide accurate results and often include features like auto-suggestions, <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/filters" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">filters</a>, and advanced search options to refine results further. This ensures users can access the information they want efficiently and effectively.SaveShare</p>

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Strategy value</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/strategy-value-1721049150782/a-1721049150782.jpg" alt="Strategy value"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/strategy-value-1721049150782/a-1721049150782-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Good information architecture should create 4 unique but interconnected types of value — strategic value, design value, user value, and technological value.<sup><a href="https://app.uxcel.com/#anchor-2">[2]</a></sup></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Strategy value relates to how well the digital interface structure aligns with the company's business goals. For instance, if a company's goal is to increase product sales, the site's content and <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/layout-composition" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">layout</a> would be designed to guide users toward making a purchase. This could involve showcasing popular products on the homepage or having a clear and easy-to-use <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/checkout" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">checkout</a> process.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Having a strong strategy value means the digital interface is not just aesthetically pleasing, but also supports and actively contributes towards the achievement of business objectives. This helps make informed decisions about what content to include and how to arrange it, ultimately resulting in a more effective and successful digital interface.SaveShare</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Design value</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/design-value-1721049199258/b-1721049199261.jpg" alt="Design value Bad Practice"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/design-value-1721049199258/b-1721049199261-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/design-value-1721049199258/a-1721049199258.jpg" alt="Design value Best Practice"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/design-value-1721049199258/a-1721049199258-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>When an interface’s IA has high design value, it means it presents information in a relevant and meaningful way for users. This involves determining what information to present, how much, and when.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Users should easily find what they need. For instance, consider a hospital's website. Patients looking for medical services need information about departments, specialists, and appointment booking. The website's IA should prioritize this content on the homepage. During a health crisis, the website should highlight emergency care contacts and detailed guidelines. Regular patients, who need access to their health records and follow-up schedules, should find these in a dedicated section. By effectively organizing and timing the presentation of this information, the website ensures that all patients can easily find what they need when they need it.SaveShare</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Value for users</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/value-for-users-1721049241812/b-1721049241814.jpg" alt="Value for users Bad Practice"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/value-for-users-1721049241812/b-1721049241814-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/value-for-users-1721049241812/a-1721049241812.jpg" alt="Value for users Best Practice"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/value-for-users-1721049241812/a-1721049241812-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>IA should simplify how users navigate, find, and use information on an interface. For instance, imagine a university's online course catalog. If the IA is well-structured, a student should easily find courses by department, professor, or course number.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The website should have clear categories like "Undergraduate Courses," "Graduate Courses," and "Online Courses." Each course listing should include essential details, such as prerequisites, schedules, and professor contact information. A robust search function should allow students to find specific courses quickly. This clear and organized structure ensures that students can efficiently plan their studies, contributing to a positive user experience. This not only benefits users by saving them time but also increases user engagement and retention for the business.SaveShare</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Technology value</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/technology-value-1721049277146/a-1721049277146.jpg" alt="Technology value"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/technology-value-1721049277146/a-1721049277146-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>IA plays a huge role not only in front-end design but also in handling data efficiently on the backend. Just like a well-designed building requires a solid foundation, a robust IA ensures that data is structured logically and stored efficiently in <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/databases" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">databases</a>. This backend organization ensures that websites and apps can quickly retrieve and process information, ultimately enhancing the overall user experience. For example, consider an e-commerce platform. With a well-structured IA on the backend, the platform can efficiently manage user accounts, product catalogs, and transaction histories. This means that users can easily access their purchase history, receive personalized recommendations, and complete transactions seamlessly.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Without proper IA, backend systems can become cluttered and inefficient, leading to slow performance, data inaccuracies, and ultimately, a poor user experience. So, investing in IA is not only about creating an intuitive front-end interface but also about optimizing data management and processing capabilities on the backend.SaveShare</p>
