<h1 class="wp-block-heading">Submitting a Form</h1>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Ensure a seamless user experience by learning how to optimize your form design</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Interaction Design</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>5 exercises</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Start&nbsp;<strong>+250px</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Forms exist to help users achieve anything from creating an account to subscribing to a newsletter. They are often the last step before users achieve their goal, so they should be quick and easy to complete. When forms consume too much time, use complicated language, or ask too personal questions, users tend to leave.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Multiple factors like alignment, structure, spacing, the design of&nbsp;<a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/buttons" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">buttons</a>&nbsp;and&nbsp;<a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/inputs" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">inputs</a>, and&nbsp;<a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/typography" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">typography</a>&nbsp;can make or break the overall experience your users have when trying to complete a form. eBay increased its annual income by 500 million dollars after it redesigned just one button on one of the mobile form screens. If you want to convert more leads and create a good impression as a user-friendly product, consider form design with great attention and respect.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Show a button to submit</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/show-button-to-submit-1640343908053/b-1682058071630.jpg" alt="Show a button to submit Bad Practice"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/show-button-to-submit-1640343908053/b-1682058071630-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/show-button-to-submit-1640343908053/a-1682058071622.jpg" alt="Show a button to submit Best Practice"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/show-button-to-submit-1640343908053/a-1682058071622-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The primary action button allowing users to submit information should always be in sight. The most intuitive placement is below the form fields or in the upper right corner. On mobile, if the form is long and users need to scroll, fix the button position so it won't disappear.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The button's <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/labels" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">label</a> should be straightforward and on-point. Users should immediately understand what happens after they press it. You can change the copy to fit the form, e.g., the button can say "Subscribe" if someone is providing their <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/email" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">email</a> address to receive emails.SaveShare</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Show loading state after submission</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/show-loading-state-after-submission-1640344243520/a-1682058132854.jpg" alt="Show loading state after submission"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/show-loading-state-after-submission-1640344243520/a-1682058132854-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/loading" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">loading</a> state helps users see the form is in the process of being submitted. According to the first of Jakob Nielsen's&nbsp;10 Heuristics<em>, </em>visibility of system status is crucial — the system should always keep users informed about what is happening right now and provide clear and on-time feedback. This makes users feel in control of the system, so they know how to act to reach their goal. Showing the button's state also increases user trust in the <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/branding" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">brand</a>.<sup><a href="https://app.uxcel.com/#anchor-1">[1]</a></sup></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Pro Tip!</strong> Make sure you also added a hover state that arises when users are about to press the button.&nbsp;SaveShare</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Show success message when it submits</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/show-success-message-when-it-submits-1640344519945/b-1682063423864-2x.mp4" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/show-success-message-when-it-submits-1640344519945/b-1682063423864-2x.mp4" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/show-success-message-when-it-submits-1640344519945/a-1682063423856-2x.mp4" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/show-success-message-when-it-submits-1640344519945/a-1682063423856-2x.mp4" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Once the form is submitted, communicate that back to users with a clear success message. First, showing a success message follows Nielsen's <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/heuristics" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">Heuristics</a> about the visibility of the system status. Second, it increases user satisfaction when they achieve one of their goals.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Pro Tip!</strong> Don't overdo it. Show success messages only when necessary and make them disappear automatically after a while.SaveShare</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Show an error message if necessary</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/if-it-doesnt-show-an-error-message-1640344642547/b-1682058286249.jpg" alt="Show an error message if necessary Bad Practice"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/if-it-doesnt-show-an-error-message-1640344642547/b-1682058286249-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/if-it-doesnt-show-an-error-message-1640344642547/a-1682058286247.jpg" alt="Show an error message if necessary Best Practice"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/if-it-doesnt-show-an-error-message-1640344642547/a-1682058286247-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Sometimes, things don't work out. If the form can't be submitted due to invalid information or another <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/errors" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">error</a>, let users know about it.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>When users see that something went wrong, they may feel disappointed, irritated, and stressed. A formal, ambiguous, robotic message is the last thing they need to see. Good error messages should be polite, clear, and informative. Clearly explain to users what happened and provide advice on what they should do next.<sup><a href="https://app.uxcel.com/#anchor-2">[2]</a></sup></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>However, providing specific error details (e.g., whether the username or <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/password" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">password</a> is incorrect) can be a security risk. Display error messages like "Invalid username or password" to prevent revealing information that could aid attackers.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Pro Tip!</strong> Avoid using technical terms to explain what caused an error. Act like a human and use simple words that users can relate to.&nbsp;SaveShare</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">An error may occur because of incorrect data</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/an-error-may-occur-because-of-wrong-information-1640344785343/a-1682058317210.jpg" alt="An error may occur because of incorrect data"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/an-error-may-occur-because-of-wrong-information-1640344785343/a-1682058317210-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>As humans, we all make mistakes, and sometimes, users provide incorrect data that doesn't meet input criteria, preventing the system from submitting the form. To value users' time, it is important to inform them about these errors promptly, instead of waiting until they hit the Submit button. Therefore, it is recommended to validate each input after users fill in the information and move the focus away from the input.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>To provide effective feedback, the error message should be concise yet precise, clearly indicating what went wrong and how users can fix the problem to continue with their task.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Pro Tip!</strong> Avoid validating each input while users are typing. It annoys people and creates mistrust of the product.&nbsp;</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<h1 class="wp-block-heading">Making a Payment</h1>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Explore best practices for designing a smooth and secure checkout experience</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Interaction Design</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>6 exercises</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Start&nbsp;<strong>+250px</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>If you're running an online business, the&nbsp;<a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/checkout" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">checkout</a>&nbsp;process is the desired destination of the selling journey. You've come a long way driving users from their first acquaintance with the product to the final stage of making a purchase. But don't relax just yet! You have to ensure that the checkout process, especially the part of providing payment details, is smooth, secure, and painless, making users feel confident that they made the right choice coming to your website.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>You should consider adding multiple payment options, provide enough feedback when users submit their confidential data, and clearly outline what's coming next after they make a payment. It'll be disappointing to lose a customer at the final stages, so take this task seriously and try to minimize any risk of confusion or uncertainty.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Offer payment methods</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/offer-payment-methods-1640345459453/b-1682058374242.jpg" alt="Offer payment methods Bad Practice"/></figure>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/offer-payment-methods-1640345459453/b-1682058374242-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/offer-payment-methods-1640345459453/a-1682058374242.jpg" alt="Offer payment methods Best Practice"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/offer-payment-methods-1640345459453/a-1682058374242-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>According to studies by the Baymard Institute, your website or mobile app should provide as many payment methods as you can if you sell internationally and don't want your users to have any trouble paying for your product/service.<sup><a href="https://app.uxcel.com/#anchor-1">[1]</a></sup> However, it's true that seeing too many unnecessary payment options confuses users and causes choice paralysis. So what's the best solution?</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Learn your audience and provide a range of payment options that are relevant to them. For example, credit cards are becoming less dominant in many countries like Germany, Russia, and China. If you don't sell to the UK, there's no point in offering BACS Direct Debit payment options.SaveShare</p>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>When requesting payment information from users, it's important to ensure that all relevant text <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/inputs" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">inputs</a> are provided and that their size is appropriate for the details being entered. Some inputs may require users to provide unfamiliar or unclear information, in which case it's helpful to offer a brief but informative explanation. One effective way to do this is to include an information <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/icons" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">icon</a> that users can click for more details.</p>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Users are humans, and they make mistakes, especially when entering payment details on mobile and using one hand. It's vital to consider adding validation before users submit their payment details. Users mistype numbers, or the card may have expired — so make sure to address these numerous error states.</p>

By displaying the current status of the system, users are more likely to trust the payment process and feel in control of the transaction. This can help increase credibility and improve the overall <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/user-experience" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">user experience</a>.SaveShare</p>

<h1 class="wp-block-heading">Contact Support</h1>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Learn how to offer clear and accessible communication channels to address user needs</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Interaction Design</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>7 exercises</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/lessons/contact-support-1705606372284-2x.svg" alt="Contact Support Lesson"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Start&nbsp;<strong>+250px</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The best way to improve&nbsp;<a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/support" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">customer support</a>&nbsp;is to eliminate the need for it. However, the nature of&nbsp;<a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/user-experience" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">UX</a>&nbsp;is such that we don't understand how successful a design is until it goes live. To make sure that users don't abandon their tasks, offer them clear ways of communication with your company.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>To provide the best support possible, you need to understand why, when, and where users need it. This will help you offer the most suitable communication methods — live&nbsp;<a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/chats" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">chat</a>, phone call, or a&nbsp;<a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/hyperlink" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">link</a>&nbsp;to F.A.Q. — in places where users expect to find them.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Collecting feedback on these&nbsp;<a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/interaction" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">interactions</a>&nbsp;will give you invaluable insights into UX failures. Implementing this knowledge to improve your product can eventually reduce the need for customer support.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Why users contact support</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/why-users-contact-support-1640346344177/a-1682058600190.jpg" alt="Why users contact support"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/why-users-contact-support-1640346344177/a-1682058600190-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>To provide the best customer support, you need to understand why users need it. <strong>According to the Nielsen Norman Group, users contact customer support in the 4 cases mentioned below in order of their frequency:</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>The information they're looking for is missing or isn't clear enough. For example, a user might contact support if they don't understand the difference between the two products offered.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>There's a service problem. For example, a payment failed or the product arrived damaged.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Users encounter roadblocks and can't finish the task. For example, users can't modify their order and must contact the representative to make changes.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>The task seems too complex. For example, if getting an insurance quote online looks too difficult, users can contact an agent instead.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In 64% of cases, the need to contact support results from a UX failure.<sup><a href="https://app.uxcel.com/#anchor-1">[1]</a></sup> But before these shortcomings can be fixed, you need to ensure that customers have all the necessary methods to get in touch with the company and finish their journey.SaveShare</p>

<p>According to <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/research-knowledge" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">research</a> by the Nielsen Norman Group, the most common method of contacting customer service is by phone, followed by <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/email" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">email</a>, online chat, and social media.<sup><a href="https://app.uxcel.com/#anchor-2">[2]</a></sup> However, not all issues require human contact, and many common problems can be resolved through a well-designed F.A.Q. section.</p>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Online chat and telephone support are ideal for specific, complex issues that require an immediate response. You can prioritize these options by placing them lower on the list of contact methods.</p>

<p>Make customer support easily accessible. As it's impossible to always predict when users will need to contact the company, offer fixed <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/links" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">links</a> that they can easily find on every <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/pages" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">page</a>. The most common places for the<strong> </strong>support page link are the <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/footers" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">footer</a> on websites and the <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/settings" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">settings</a> page in mobile apps.</p>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/errors" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">Error</a> pages are places where you can predict the need for customer support. Offering assistance at the point of error can ensure that users complete their tasks successfully.</p>

<!-- wp:heading {"level":1} -->
<h1 class="wp-block-heading">Deleting Account</h1>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Explore how to handle user departures in a respectful and professional manner</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Interaction Design</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>8 exercises</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Start&nbsp;<strong>+250px</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Sometimes, users decide they don't want to use your product or service — and that's okay. Users come and go, and it's important to understand that your product can't serve all users' needs and solve all users' problems. Some companies combine two methods of exit — deactivating an account and permanently deleting it. Deactivating allows users to restore their accounts during a certain period (a week or 30 days) and permanently deletes them after this period ends.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>For those users who want to leave, make it as simple as possible. You can try to understand why they're leaving, but don't be clingy. Whatever you do, avoid being passive-aggressive or guilt-tripping. It's unprofessional and makes you look desperate. Plus, users will remember this and may share their experience with others who will reconsider your product.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Show a link to delete the account</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/show-a-link-to-delete-the-account-1640347176519/b-1682058864493.jpg" alt="Show a link to delete the account Bad Practice"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/show-a-link-to-delete-the-account-1640347176519/b-1682058864493-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/show-a-link-to-delete-the-account-1640347176519/a-1682058864493.jpg" alt="Show a link to delete the account Best Practice"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/show-a-link-to-delete-the-account-1640347176519/a-1682058864493-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Making it difficult for users to delete their account by hiding the "Delete Account" <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/hyperlink" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">link</a> creates a negative impression that may limit users' trust in your product and harm your reputation. Therefore, the <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/links" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">link</a> or <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/buttons" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">button</a> to delete the account should be visible and easily accessible on the profile, <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/settings" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">settings</a> <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/pages" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">page</a>, or even in the support area. By doing so, users feel in control of their data, which can increase their confidence and trust in your product.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Pro Tip!</strong> However, you shouldn't encourage destructive actions. Place the Delete Account link in a safe area where users can find it but not click it accidentally.&nbsp;SaveShare</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Preserve goodwill</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/preserve-goodwill-1640347249212/b-1682058897120.jpg" alt="Preserve goodwill Bad Practice"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/preserve-goodwill-1640347249212/b-1682058897120-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/preserve-goodwill-1640347249212/a-1682058897119.jpg" alt="Preserve goodwill Best Practice"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/preserve-goodwill-1640347249212/a-1682058897119-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>People don't always delete accounts because they're unhappy. Sometimes they just no longer need the service because they completed their task or found what they needed. Preserving goodwill and avoiding a blaming tone is the best policy to save your product's reputation. It also means loyalty — users are more likely to give positive referrals in the future or come back to the product later on.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Hiding the Unsubscribe or Delete Account buttons or using other <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/dark-pattern" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">dark patterns</a> to retain users at any cost never helps. If users want to leave, they will leave eventually. Your task is to create a good impression and demonstrate decent behavior while allowing them to do so.SaveShare</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Offer alternatives</h2>

<h1 class="wp-block-heading">Showing Progress</h1>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Learn how to correctly use progress trackers to enhance user experience</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Interaction Design</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>6 exercises</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Start&nbsp;<strong>+250px</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/progress-trackers" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">Progress trackers</a>&nbsp;are determinate indicators used to represent where users are in a journey. They inform them what they have completed, where they are currently, and what's left to complete. "Visibility of system status" is one of the most important&nbsp;<a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/usability-heuristics" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">usability heuristics</a>, which helps reduce users' tension and uncertainty.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The power of showing progress is that it provides ongoing feedback to users. It also carves a clear path to completion for them, which increases the likelihood of them making it to the end of the journey.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">When to use progress trackers</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/when-to-use-progress-trackers-1640347941319/a-1682059234280.jpg" alt="When to use progress trackers"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/when-to-use-progress-trackers-1640347941319/a-1682059234280-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Progress trackers are a valuable feature that can improve the <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/user-experience" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">user experience</a> by breaking a long form into logical and numbered steps, allowing users to track their progress. They can be especially helpful in minimizing user abandonment rates and motivating users to move forward and complete a task. By providing a clear sense of progress, progress trackers can satisfy the basic psychological need for closure and increase user satisfaction when a form is completed successfully.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>On the other hand, ambiguous and confusing forms can create roadblocks, impede progress, and cause frustration for users. Therefore, using progress trackers can improve the user experience and increase the likelihood of successful form completion.<sup><a href="https://app.uxcel.com/#anchor-1">[1]</a></sup></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In addition to tracking progress, progress trackers provide users with information about how many steps they have completed, their current position, and the remaining steps. However, it's important to note that not all forms require progress trackers. Overcomplicating the flow of a form can be counterproductive, and progress trackers should only be used when the process is long and takes more than three steps.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Forms such as <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/checkout" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">checkout</a> processes, tax forms, flight bookings, scholarship applications, and other complex forms that require multiple steps can benefit from progress trackers. By providing users with a clear sense of their progress, progress trackers can encourage users to continue and ultimately lead to a more positive user experience.SaveShare</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Determine your progress tracker type</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/determine-your-type-of-progress-tracker-1640348037073/b-1682059277404.jpg" alt="Determine your progress tracker type Bad Practice"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/determine-your-type-of-progress-tracker-1640348037073/b-1682059277404-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/determine-your-type-of-progress-tracker-1640348037073/a-1682059277403.jpg" alt="Determine your progress tracker type Best Practice"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/determine-your-type-of-progress-tracker-1640348037073/a-1682059277403-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The tracker can exist as determinate progress bars or numbered steps. Their main goal is to show users the current progress, how many steps are ahead, and how many have been completed. Progress trackers help encourage users to keep going and complete a specific task.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>When the form is large and includes at least 3 stages, numbered steps are more helpful for setting milestones. Simply seeing the last step can motivate users to go through each step and complete a form. When the journey isn't long, like in the case of a <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/signup" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">signup</a> form, it can be enough to show the incremental filling of a bar as users progress.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Pro Tip!</strong> You can encounter progress trackers when purchasing online, filling in a multistep form (like booking a flight), or taking an onboarding tour.SaveShare</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">The importance of providing feedback to users</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/the-importance-of-providing-feedback-to-users-1640348167197/a-1682059326121.jpg" alt="The importance of providing feedback to users"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/the-importance-of-providing-feedback-to-users-1640348167197/a-1682059326121-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>One of the primary goals of using progress trackers is to notify users about what is happening in the system when they fill in a form. Users may get distracted, leave the window, and get back to the form later. They should immediately understand what they're doing, their current status, how many steps they've already gone through, and how many steps are waiting for them ahead.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Progress trackers support Nielsen's "visibility of system status" <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/heuristics" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">heuristic</a>. When users know what's going on in the system, they feel in control and don't experience any fear of the unknown.<sup><a href="https://app.uxcel.com/#anchor-2">[2]</a></sup>&nbsp;Plus, people will be more likely to complete the process if they know what's ahead of them and have a sense of completion along the way.SaveShare</p>

<h1 class="wp-block-heading">Resetting Password</h1>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Explore techniques to streamline the password reset flow and minimize user effort</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Start&nbsp;<strong>+250px</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>On average, people use 4-5 passwords per day. Due to hard security rules, users keep forgetting their passwords to log back in.<sup><a href="https://app.uxcel.com/#anchor-1">[1]</a></sup>&nbsp;In these moments, users may experience disappointment, annoyance, or even guilt. In fact, 75% of online customers won't complete their purchase if they fail to recover a&nbsp;<a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/password" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">password</a>&nbsp;while checking out.<sup><a href="https://app.uxcel.com/#anchor-2">[2]</a></sup></p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Prevent errors from the start</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/prevent-errors-from-the-start-1640349671250/b-1682059711440.jpg" alt="Prevent errors from the start Bad Practice"/></figure>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/prevent-errors-from-the-start-1640349671250/b-1682059711440-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/prevent-errors-from-the-start-1640349671250/a-1682059711439.jpg" alt="Prevent errors from the start Best Practice"/></figure>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/prevent-errors-from-the-start-1640349671250/a-1682059711439-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In the ideal world, users never make mistakes and complete tasks successfully every time. Unfortunately, in the real world, systems can be confusing, users may get distracted or lose attention, and errors happen.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Error-prevention strategies for <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/inputs" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">inputs</a> include:</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Using constraints: </strong>Constraints prevent users from typing a wrong input by setting boundaries. For example, inputs asking for users' phone numbers or banking card numbers should show a numeric keyboard only.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Using forgiving formats: </strong>Rather than forcing users to type in a format desired for your system, allow them to type in a way that's convenient to them. Then, format their input while users are typing and add necessary spaces, parentheses, or hyphens, removing letters if it's a phone number input, for example.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Reducing cognitive load: </strong>Don't force users to keep too many things in mind. For example, it's a good practice to detect the SMS passcode from their messages and display it above the keyboard. This way, users don't need to switch between screens and strain their memory.<sup><a href="https://app.uxcel.com/#anchor-1">[1]</a></sup></li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Providing clear instructions: </strong>Users don't like reading and prefer skimming information. So make sure <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/labels" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">labels</a>, <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/tooltips" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">tooltips</a>, and form instructions are concise and straight to the point.</li>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Meaningful error messages</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/meaningful-error-messages-1640350276461/b-1682059898723.jpg" alt="Meaningful error messages Bad Practice"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/meaningful-error-messages-1640350276461/b-1682059898723-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/meaningful-error-messages-1640350276461/a-1682059898723.jpg" alt="Meaningful error messages Best Practice"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/meaningful-error-messages-1640350276461/a-1682059898723-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Even if you take all measures to prevent input errors, users will make them anyway. Providing meaningful error messages reassures users that mistakes happen and that it isn't the end of the world.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Use plain language: </strong>Get rid of technical jargon and passive voices, which may confuse users.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Keep the message short: </strong>Users don't like to read, and in the face of a stressful environment, they are even less likely to read something longer than 1-2 sentences. A study by the American Press Institute shows that users are more likely to understand the whole piece of information only if the sentence contains no more than 8 words.<sup><a href="https://app.uxcel.com/#anchor-4">[4]</a></sup></li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Be precise: </strong>Avoid ambiguous phrases like "an error occurred," as they don't help users identify the root cause. Tell them which input contains the error and what they are expected to enter in order to solve it.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Be polite: </strong>Avoid blaming users or making them feel stupid. Focus on fixing the problem and building trust and loyalty.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>SaveShare</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Keep the error state visible till fixed</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Keeping the error state visible while the user is making corrections is important because:</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>It maintains context about what needs to be fixed</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Users don't have to remember what the error was</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>It provides immediate feedback as they work toward a solution</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>It prevents confusion about whether the error has actually been resolved</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The field should only return to its default or success state when the input actually becomes valid, not just when the user starts typing again. For example, if a password is too short, the error state and message should persist until the password reaches the minimum length requirement.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The only visual change during user input should be real-time validation feedback, like checkmarks appearing next to requirements as they're met.</p>

<p>Placeholders are supposed to clarify what information users need to enter and increase the form completion speed. Unfortunately, many people confuse placeholders with real data filled in by default.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Plus, if placeholders substitute labels, they disappear after users place the <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/cursors" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">cursor</a> in the input. If they get distracted at this time, they will need to shift their focus away from the input to display the <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/placeholder" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">placeholder</a> text again.<sup><a href="https://app.uxcel.com/#anchor-3">[3]</a></sup> If placeholders are so tricky, how can we use them to help users prevent errors?</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Input masks are an excellent solution when formatting matters (for things like phone numbers or dates). Besides displaying the desired input format, they also automatically apply it to users' input as they type. This tool reduces <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/cognitiveload" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">cognitive load</a> and confusion, so users don't need to worry and double-check if they have typed everything correctly.SaveShare</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Once users move away from the input field by selecting another element or removing the cursor, the system can begin validating the input. In case an error occurs, display a message next to the input and explain how users can correct it. <h2 class="wp-block-heading">Meaningful error messages</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/meaningful-error-messages-1640350276461/b-1682059898723.jpg" alt="Meaningful error messages Bad Practice"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/meaningful-error-messages-1640350276461/b-1682059898723-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/meaningful-error-messages-1640350276461/a-1682059898723.jpg" alt="Meaningful error messages Best Practice"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/meaningful-error-messages-1640350276461/a-1682059898723-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Even if you take all measures to prevent input errors, users will make them anyway. Providing meaningful error messages reassures users that mistakes happen and that it isn't the end of the world.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Use plain language: </strong>Get rid of technical jargon and passive voices, which may confuse users.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Keep the message short: </strong>Users don't like to read, and in the face of a stressful environment, they are even less likely to read something longer than 1-2 sentences. A study by the American Press Institute shows that users are more likely to understand the whole piece of information only if the sentence contains no more than 8 words.<sup><a href="https://app.uxcel.com/#anchor-4">[4]</a></sup></li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Be precise: </strong>Avoid ambiguous phrases like "an error occurred," as they don't help users identify the root cause. Tell them which input contains the error and what they are expected to enter in order to solve it.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Be polite: </strong>Avoid blaming users or making them feel stupid. Focus on fixing the problem and building trust and loyalty.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>SaveShare</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Keep the error state visible till fixed</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Keeping the error state visible while the user is making corrections is important because:</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>It maintains context about what needs to be fixed</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Users don't have to remember what the error was</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>It provides immediate feedback as they work toward a solution</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>It prevents confusion about whether the error has actually been resolved</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The field should only return to its default or success state when the input actually becomes valid, not just when the user starts typing again. For example, if a password is too short, the error state and message should persist until the password reaches the minimum length requirement.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The only visual change during user input should be real-time validation feedback, like checkmarks appearing next to requirements as they're met.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">When to autosave</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/when-to-autosave-1640352077866/a-1682062429726.jpg" alt="When to autosave"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/when-to-autosave-1640352077866/a-1682062429726-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Autosave is becoming more of a common practice within applications. If done correctly, it can improve the <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/user-experience" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">user experience</a>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The most common <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/use-case" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">use cases</a> for this are <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/settings" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">settings</a> that are easy to revert, such as toggles and <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/checkboxes" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">checkboxes</a>. This way, users can just change one setting without reviewing all of them. It also feels intuitive — we expect the setting to work as soon as we flick the switch, the same as when we turn on a light. Even if you enable autosave, allow users to also save changes manually with the Save Changes button.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Pro Tip!</strong> When considering autosave, analyze the platform your target audience uses. For example, macOS users are used to autosaving while Windows users aren't.SaveShare</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Canceling or reverting changes</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/canceling-or-reverting-changes-1640353027131/b-1682062459749.jpg" alt="Canceling or reverting changes Bad Practice"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/canceling-or-reverting-changes-1640353027131/b-1682062459749-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/canceling-or-reverting-changes-1640353027131/a-1682062459748.jpg" alt="Canceling or reverting changes Best Practice"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/canceling-or-reverting-changes-1640353027131/a-1682062459748-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Allow users to leave without making changes. Most users expect the changes not to be saved when they close the window or press the Back button, so avoid tying autosaving to these functions.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>You can use a confirmation dialogue when users modify important information such as an email address or phone number without saving it. Make sure that the dialogue is short and straightforward. The headline should ask, or inform, about one main action we'd like to reconfirm. Avoid generic headlines for confirmation dialogues like "Warning" or "Are you sure."</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Use unambiguous CTA buttons. The word Cancel isn't great when related to deleting or removing, as it's not clear what is canceled — the action or the cancellation of the action. Better options include Back, Stay, No, Leave, Quit, or any other action word that is instinctively clear in context.</p>

<h1 class="wp-block-heading">Canceling Subscription</h1>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Explore the importance of providing a graceful exit for users who decide to cancel their accounts or stop using your product or service</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Abreakup is never something you look forward to or enjoy. Similar to closing an account, users can decide to end their experience and stop paying for your product. Well, users leave, but it's not a tragedy, and it doesn't mean you can't give them a graceful exit. It's important to make a cancellation obvious to find and easy to travel through. Remember, a decent and polite goodbye can set the stage for users to come back in the future.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Besides, it's a great opportunity to receive users' feedback and use it for product improvement.</p>

<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/show-a-link-in-account-details-1640352216474/a-1690359673694.jpg" alt="Make the Unsubscribe button easy to locate Best Practice"/></figure>

<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Hiding the Unsubscribe button can lead to low loyalty and <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/branding" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">brand</a> mistrust. Be honest and respectful, allowing users to decide for themselves whether they want to use your product or want to stop using it forever or temporarily.SaveShare</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Confirm intent to cancel</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image -->

<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/confirm-intent-to-cancel-1640352290826/a-1682062566543-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Sometimes, users may accidentally click the destructive button that triggers the removal of an account or subscription. So prevent irretrievable actions by asking users to confirm their choice. Plus, it's an additional opportunity to try and reel users back by pointing out what they'll lose when they cancel the subscription. For example, if users can access the product only with a subscription, you can remind them of the product's value.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Conversely, if the product can be accessed in a free version, you can remind users of some specific subscription benefits. The golden rule here is to avoid using language that begs for sympathy or exploits tools of pressure or manipulation.SaveShare</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Request a reason for canceling</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/request-a-reason-for-canceling-1640352364553/a-1682062586982.jpg" alt="Request a reason for canceling"/></figure>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/request-a-reason-for-canceling-1640352364553/a-1682062586982-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>When users decide to cancel their subscription, it's a great opportunity to receive feedback and learn what can be improved. Was the product too expensive? Did users prefer a competitor? Did they get what they needed and no longer need the service?</p>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Don't make this question mandatory and avoid pressing users to provide a detailed answer. Be polite and provide possible options for why users may want to leave. Additionally, include a section labeled "Other" that allows users to provide their own reasons for leaving in an open-ended format.</p>
