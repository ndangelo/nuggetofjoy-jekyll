<!-- wp:heading {"level":4} -->
<h4 class="wp-block-heading" id="heading-level-4">Replace the resume text with your own</h4>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Open the supplied creative-resume-design_START.indd file. With the Type tool, highlight the text in any text box and type your own text to replace it. You can delete a block of text if you don’t need it or copy and paste additional text.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Repeat for all sections of the resume.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><img src="https://helpx.adobe.com/content/dam/help/en/indesign/how-to/creative-resume-design/jcr_content/main-pars/image/creative-resume-design-step1-900x506.jpg" alt="Graphic"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":4} -->
<h4 class="wp-block-heading" id="heading-level-4">Quickly format text with styles</h4>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>As you type, you’ll notice that the text is already formatted. Our resume contains predefined paragraph styles — styles save various formatting attributes and make it a snap to change the appearance of text in your document.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Note:</strong>&nbsp;Normally, a new document starts out with one default style. You can create additional styles to keep track of your text formatting.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>You can use these styles as they are or change some attributes and redefine the styles. Try changing the look of the headings.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Select a heading with the Type tool. In the Text Style section of the Properties panel, you’ll notice that the “Headings” paragraph style is applied to it.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Choose a different font, style, and font size from the Character section. Click the More Options button (three dots) and click the All Caps button.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><img src="https://helpx.adobe.com/content/dam/help/en/indesign/how-to/creative-resume-design/jcr_content/main-pars/image_2083894552/creative-resume-design-step2a-900x506.jpg" alt="Graphic"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The “Headings” paragraph style name now shows a “+,” showing the text now has different formatting than the Heading style.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Click the small Redefine Styles button just below the style name to update the Headings style and apply your changes to all the headings in the document formatted with this style.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><img src="https://helpx.adobe.com/content/dam/help/en/indesign/how-to/creative-resume-design/jcr_content/main-pars/image_1949119704/creative-resume-design-step2b-900x506.jpg" alt="Graphic"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":4} -->
<h4 class="wp-block-heading" id="heading-level-4">Save your own text style</h4>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Now you’ll create a paragraph style to make a small edit throughout the document without laboring to change each instance manually.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Select some text with the Type tool. Increase the font size in the Character section.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In the Text Style section of the Properties panel, click the New Paragraph Style button (+) and give the style a name. We called it “H3.”</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Now select other text individually and apply your new text style by choosing it in the Paragraph Styles menu in the Text Style section.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><img src="https://helpx.adobe.com/content/dam/help/en/indesign/how-to/creative-resume-design/jcr_content/main-pars/image_1704547130/creative-resume-design-step3-900x506.jpg" alt="Graphic"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":4} -->
<h4 class="wp-block-heading" id="heading-level-4">Add some personality with a headshot</h4>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Select the black frame with the Selection tool. Choose File &gt; Place and choose your own image or use the supplied sample file. Click Open.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>If your image is too large for the frame, you can resize it to fit. Try clicking one of the fitting options in the Frame Fitting section of the Properties panel.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>To reposition the image, hover over the image until you see the Content Grabber (donut shape) and then drag to position the image in the frame.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><img src="https://helpx.adobe.com/content/dam/help/en/indesign/how-to/creative-resume-design/jcr_content/main-pars/image_1851015676/creative-resume-design-step4-900x506.jpg" alt="Graphic"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":4} -->
<h4 class="wp-block-heading" id="heading-level-4">Use colors from your photo</h4>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>You can sample a color from your photo to tie to other design elements. To apply a color to the large graphic element, you’ll have to unlock it first. Choose Object &gt; Unlock All on Spread. This step selects the shape in the background.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Select the Eyedropper tool and click an area of the photo. To protect the graphic element from any accidental changes, click Lock in the Quick Actions section of the Properties panel.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><img src="https://helpx.adobe.com/content/dam/help/en/indesign/how-to/creative-resume-design/jcr_content/main-pars/image_2092932403/creative-resume-design-step5-900x506.jpg" alt="Graphic"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":4} -->
<h4 class="wp-block-heading" id="heading-level-4">Add finishing touches</h4>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Finally, adjust the skill bars at the bottom to show your expertise at a glance.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>With the Selection tool, click a white rectangle. Then drag in either direction to increase or decrease the value.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Tip:</strong>&nbsp;With the Zoom tool, click in the area to enlarge your view. When you’re done, double-click the Zoom tool in the Toolbar to return to 100% view.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><img src="https://helpx.adobe.com/content/dam/help/en/indesign/how-to/creative-resume-design/jcr_content/main-pars/image_1098957072/creative-resume-design-step6-900x506.jpg" alt="Graphic"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":4} -->
<h4 class="wp-block-heading" id="heading-level-4">Share your resume with the world</h4>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Choose File &gt; Export &gt; Adobe PDF (Print) and click Save.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In the Adobe PDF Preset menu, choose the High Quality Print preset.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In the Marks And Bleeds category, select:</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Crop Marks and Bleed Marks in the Marks section.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Use Document Bleed Settings in the Bleed And Slug section.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Click Export to save a print-ready PDF that you can email or upload online.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Note:</strong>&nbsp;Since the background runs to the edge of the paper, we saved our sample file with a bleed. The bleed extends the design slightly beyond the page edges so that no white edges show when the cards are printed and trimmed. See&nbsp;<a href="https://helpx.adobe.com/indesign/how-to/set-print-bleed.html" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">How to set a print bleed</a>&nbsp;to learn more.</p>
