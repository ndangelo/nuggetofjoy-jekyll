<p>Graphic designers owe a huge amount to the legacy of the Bauhaus, a German art school that was one of the most influential design movements to emerge in Europe during the pre-war period. The designers and architects who were part of the Bauhaus went on to define some of the most recognizable traits of modernist graphic design.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Here, we’ll look at how to create a tribute poster, using typography, colors, and grids, which are trademarks of the Bauhaus style. This is a great introduction to Bauhaus poster design in InDesign for beginners. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="toc-mncl-what-is-the-bauhaus">What Is the Bauhaus?</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Before we begin to design our InDesign poster, let’s take a quick look at what the Bauhaus was, and its lasting influence on design today.&nbsp;</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Bauhaus was a revolutionary school of art, architecture and design established by Walter Gropius at Weimar in Germany in 1919. The name Bauhaus combines the German words for building (bau) and house (haus), which might have hinted that the founders wanted to create a guild or fraternity that would work towards building a new and different German society.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The influence of Bauhaus was widespread across many different design disciplines, establishing new and exciting ways of approaching modernist architecture, interiors, art, photography, and graphics. The school moved to Dessau in Germany in 1925 and finally to Berlin in 1932, before closing in 1933.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In this InDesign poster tutorial, the focus is on the primary colors frequently adopted by Bauhaus designers and sans serif typography, a defining feature of Bauhaus-era graphic design. We'll also be learning how to make a poster using InDesign in general! You'll be able to apply these techniques to different designs.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="toc-kyav-how-to-set-up-your-poster-document-in-indesign">1.&nbsp;How to Set Up the File for Your Poster in InDesign</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="toc-5n2o-step-1">Step 1</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Open&nbsp;<strong>InDesign</strong>&nbsp;and go to&nbsp;<strong>File &gt; New &gt; Document.&nbsp;</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Choose&nbsp;<strong>A3</strong>&nbsp;for the page size, and deselect&nbsp;<strong>Facing Pages</strong>. Add&nbsp;<strong>10 mm Margins</strong>&nbsp;and a&nbsp;<strong>5 mm Bleed</strong>, before clicking&nbsp;<strong>Create</strong>.&nbsp;</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><img src="https://cms-assets.tutsplus.com/cdn-cgi/image/width=850/uploads/users/114/posts/31416/image/1.1.jpg" alt="new document"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="toc-3bci-step-2">Step 2</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Expand the&nbsp;<strong>Layers</strong>&nbsp;panel<strong>&nbsp;(Window &gt; Layers)&nbsp;</strong>and double-click on&nbsp;<strong><em>Layer 1</em></strong>, renaming it&nbsp;<strong><em>Background</em></strong>.&nbsp;</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Choose&nbsp;<strong>New Layer</strong>&nbsp;from the panel’s drop-down menu, and name this second layer&nbsp;<strong><em>Texture.</em></strong>&nbsp;</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Create three more new layers—<strong><em>Guides, Shapes,</em></strong>&nbsp;and&nbsp;<strong><em>Type</em></strong>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><img src="https://cms-assets.tutsplus.com/cdn-cgi/image/width=850/uploads/users/114/posts/31416/image/1.2.jpg" alt="layer options"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Lock all the layers except&nbsp;<strong><em>Guides</em></strong>, which we’ll work on first.&nbsp;</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><img src="https://cms-assets.tutsplus.com/cdn-cgi/image/width=850/uploads/users/114/posts/31416/image/1.2.1.jpg" alt="unlocked layer"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="toc-b3g5-step-3">Step 3</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Working on the&nbsp;<strong><em>Guides</em></strong>&nbsp;layer, pull down a guide from the top ruler&nbsp;<strong>(View &gt; Show Rulers)</strong>&nbsp;to the center point of the page, at<strong>&nbsp;210 mm.</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Drag a second across from the left-hand ruler to the vertical center-point, at&nbsp;<strong>148.5 mm.&nbsp;</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><img src="https://cms-assets.tutsplus.com/cdn-cgi/image/width=850/uploads/users/114/posts/31416/image/1.3.jpg" alt="guides layer"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="toc-0xdb-step-4">Step 4</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Select the&nbsp;<strong>Line Tool (\)</strong>&nbsp;and, holding&nbsp;<strong>Shift</strong>, drag to create a vertical line running on top of the guide below.&nbsp;</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><img src="https://cms-assets.tutsplus.com/cdn-cgi/image/width=850/uploads/users/114/posts/31416/image/1.4.jpg" alt="vertical guide"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Then create a second line over the top of the horizontal guide.&nbsp;</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><img src="https://cms-assets.tutsplus.com/cdn-cgi/image/width=850/uploads/users/114/posts/31416/image/1.4.1.jpg" alt="guide"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="toc-qeey-step-5">Step 5</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Select both lines and&nbsp;<strong>Right-Click &gt; Transform &gt; Rotate.</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><img src="https://cms-assets.tutsplus.com/cdn-cgi/image/width=850/uploads/users/114/posts/31416/image/1.5.jpg" alt="transform rotate"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Type in&nbsp;<strong>45</strong>&nbsp;and click&nbsp;<strong>OK</strong>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><img src="https://cms-assets.tutsplus.com/cdn-cgi/image/width=850/uploads/users/114/posts/31416/image/1.5.1.jpg" alt="rotate"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>You can use these guides as a basis for making sure your text frames are lined up at 45-degree angles. It’s easy to copy and paste them and create more guides as you work.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><img src="https://cms-assets.tutsplus.com/cdn-cgi/image/width=850/uploads/users/114/posts/31416/image/1.5.2.jpg" alt="guides rotated"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="toc-crrj-how-to-format-typography-on-your-poster">2.&nbsp;How to Format Typography on Your Bauhaus Poster Design</h2>

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="toc-slih-step-1">Step 1</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Lock the&nbsp;<strong><em>Background</em></strong>&nbsp;layer and unlock the&nbsp;<strong><em>Type</em></strong>&nbsp;layer at the top.&nbsp;</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Use the&nbsp;<strong>Type Tool (T)</strong>&nbsp;to create a large text frame, before&nbsp;<strong>Right-Clicking &gt; Transform &gt; Rotate</strong>, and setting the angle to&nbsp;<strong>-45</strong>&nbsp;degrees.&nbsp;</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Type in ‘bauhaus’ and from either the top&nbsp;<strong>Controls</strong>&nbsp;panel or the&nbsp;<strong>Character</strong>&nbsp;panel<strong>&nbsp;(Window &gt; Type &amp; Tables &gt; Character)</strong>, set the&nbsp;<strong>Font</strong>&nbsp;to&nbsp;<strong>Bergen Sans Bold, Size 200 pt.&nbsp;</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><img src="https://cms-assets.tutsplus.com/cdn-cgi/image/width=850/uploads/users/114/posts/31416/image/2.1.jpg" alt="bauhaus text frame"/></figure>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="toc-p5s8-step-2">Step 2</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Create a second text frame, aligning it along the bottom-right guide (rotate this to a 45-degree angle).</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Type in a sentence about the Bauhaus, either taken from the image below or using your own text. Set the&nbsp;<strong>Font</strong>&nbsp;to&nbsp;<strong>Bergen Sans Regular, Size 27 pt.</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><img src="https://cms-assets.tutsplus.com/cdn-cgi/image/width=850/uploads/users/114/posts/31416/image/2.2.jpg" alt="bergen sans"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>You can pull out any key words in a&nbsp;<strong>Bold</strong>&nbsp;weight.&nbsp;</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><img src="https://cms-assets.tutsplus.com/cdn-cgi/image/width=850/uploads/users/114/posts/31416/image/2.2.1.jpg" alt="bold weight"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="toc-rata-step-3">Step 3</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Add another text frame to the bottom-left part of the layout, resting the last line of text on the guide.&nbsp;</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Set the&nbsp;<strong>Font</strong>&nbsp;of this to&nbsp;<strong>Regime, Size 23 pt.&nbsp;</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><img src="https://cms-assets.tutsplus.com/cdn-cgi/image/width=850/uploads/users/114/posts/31416/image/2.3.jpg" alt="regim font"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="toc-zv2j-step-4">Step 4</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Add a small text frame to the top-right of the page, typing in the dates of the Bauhaus movement, ‘1919 - 1933’.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Set the&nbsp;<strong>Font</strong>&nbsp;to&nbsp;<strong>Regime Regular, Size 60 pt.&nbsp;</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><img src="https://cms-assets.tutsplus.com/cdn-cgi/image/width=850/uploads/users/114/posts/31416/image/1.jpg" alt="regime"/></figure>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Add a final text frame above and parallel to ‘bauhaus’, typing in a subtitle, such as ‘German Art School’. Set the&nbsp;<strong>Font</strong>&nbsp;to&nbsp;<strong>Bergen Sans Regular, Size 70 pt.&nbsp;</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><img src="https://cms-assets.tutsplus.com/cdn-cgi/image/width=850/uploads/users/114/posts/31416/image/2.jpg" alt="bergen sans"/></figure>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="toc-cb2p-how-to-add-a-vintage-texture-to-your-layout">4.&nbsp;How to Add a Vintage Texture to Your Bauhaus Poster Design</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="toc-w6oa-step-1">Step 1</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Lock the&nbsp;<strong><em>Shapes</em></strong>&nbsp;layer and unlock the&nbsp;<strong><em>Texture</em></strong>&nbsp;layer.&nbsp;</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Use the&nbsp;<strong>Rectangle Frame Tool (F)</strong>&nbsp;to create a frame across the whole page.&nbsp;<strong>File &gt; Place</strong>, navigate to the&nbsp;<a href="https://elements.envato.com/vintage-paper-textures-volume-2-Q3FJ56" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">Vintage Paper Texture Pack</a>, and choose&nbsp;<strong>‘Texture_1’</strong>. Click&nbsp;<strong>Open</strong>, and allow it to fill up the frame.&nbsp;</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><img src="https://cms-assets.tutsplus.com/cdn-cgi/image/width=850/uploads/users/114/posts/31416/image/14.jpg" alt="texture layer"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>With the image frame selected, go to&nbsp;<strong>Object &gt; Effects &gt; Transparency</strong>&nbsp;and bring the&nbsp;<strong>Opacity</strong>&nbsp;down to&nbsp;<strong>60%</strong>. Click&nbsp;<strong>OK</strong>.&nbsp;</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><img src="https://cms-assets.tutsplus.com/cdn-cgi/image/width=850/uploads/users/114/posts/31416/image/15.jpg" alt="opacity"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="toc-gxl5-step-2">Step 2</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Create a shape over the top of the texture using the&nbsp;<strong>Rectangle Tool (M),</strong>&nbsp;setting the&nbsp;<strong>Fill</strong>&nbsp;to&nbsp;<strong>C=36 M=31 Y=33 K=10.&nbsp;</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><img src="https://cms-assets.tutsplus.com/cdn-cgi/image/width=850/uploads/users/114/posts/31416/image/16.jpg" alt="circle grey"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Go to&nbsp;<strong>Object &gt; Effects &gt; Transparency</strong>&nbsp;and set the&nbsp;<strong>Opacity</strong>&nbsp;of this to&nbsp;<strong>35%.&nbsp;</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><img src="https://cms-assets.tutsplus.com/cdn-cgi/image/width=850/uploads/users/114/posts/31416/image/17.jpg" alt="opacity"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Click on&nbsp;<strong>Gradient Feather</strong>&nbsp;at the bottom of the&nbsp;<strong>Effects</strong>&nbsp;window’s left-hand menu.&nbsp;</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Set the&nbsp;<strong>Type</strong>&nbsp;to&nbsp;<strong>Radial</strong>&nbsp;and adjust the&nbsp;<strong>Gradient Stops</strong>&nbsp;so that the gradient extends from lightest towards the center of the page, and darker towards the outer edges. Click&nbsp;<strong>OK</strong>.&nbsp;</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="toc-9ia9-how-to-export-your-poster-design-for-printing">5.&nbsp;How to Export Your Poster in InDesign for Printing</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Your Bauhaus poster design is finished—great job! All that’s left to do now is export it to a usable format. For sharing online, you can simply go to&nbsp;<strong>File &gt; Export</strong>&nbsp;and choose&nbsp;<strong>JPEG</strong>&nbsp;or&nbsp;<strong>PNG</strong>&nbsp;from the&nbsp;<strong>Format</strong>&nbsp;options available. This will be an essential step when learning how to create a poster in InDesign.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>If you’re planning on getting your Bauhaus posters printed professionally, follow the instructions below.&nbsp;</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="toc-dcw1-step-1">Step 1</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Go to&nbsp;<strong>File &gt; Export.</strong>&nbsp;Name your file, and choose&nbsp;<strong>Adobe PDF (Print)</strong>&nbsp;from the&nbsp;<strong>Format</strong>&nbsp;menu at the bottom of the window. Click&nbsp;<strong>Save</strong>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In the&nbsp;<strong>Export Adobe PDF</strong>&nbsp;window that opens, choose&nbsp;<strong>[Press Quality]&nbsp;</strong>from the&nbsp;<strong>Preset</strong>&nbsp;menu at the top.&nbsp;</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><img src="https://cms-assets.tutsplus.com/cdn-cgi/image/width=850/uploads/users/114/posts/31416/image/5.1.jpg" alt="press quality"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="toc-lrwn-step-2">Step 2</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Click on&nbsp;<strong>Marks and Bleeds</strong>&nbsp;in the window’s left-hand menu.&nbsp;</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Check both&nbsp;<strong>All Printer’s Marks</strong>&nbsp;and&nbsp;<strong>Use Document Bleed Settings</strong>, before clicking&nbsp;<strong>Export</strong>.&nbsp;</p>
