<h2 class="wp-block-heading">'Continue with...' step</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image {"linkDestination":"custom"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><a href="https://users-content.uxcel.com/2318dc26-2162-42fc-92ba-cd4266e7ed81/briefs/reset-password-mobile-app-flow-8769-1721158883902.png" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener"><img src="https://users-content.uxcel.com/2318dc26-2162-42fc-92ba-cd4266e7ed81/briefs/reset-password-mobile-app-flow-8769-1721158883902.png" alt=""/></a></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>This flow is for a user who has an existing email address, so a ‘Continue with email’ button is used to demonstrate how that process looks.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Most people sign up with social or third party options, so they appear first in the hierarchy. These are grouped together to show that they are separate options from the ‘continue with email’ below.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>A good reason to use all of the ‘continue with...’ buttons up front, rather than ‘log in’ and ‘register’ buttons, is successive pages can be more minimal and focused in their design without the distraction of additional options peppered throughout the process.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">'Enter email' step</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image {"linkDestination":"custom"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><a href="https://users-content.uxcel.com/2318dc26-2162-42fc-92ba-cd4266e7ed81/briefs/reset-password-mobile-app-flow-8686-1721158883902.png" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener"><img src="https://users-content.uxcel.com/2318dc26-2162-42fc-92ba-cd4266e7ed81/briefs/reset-password-mobile-app-flow-8686-1721158883902.png" alt=""/></a></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>When landing on this page, the email field is auto-focused so that the keyboard is immediately accessible.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>When the user enters their email, the system checks if an account exists, if the account exists then the system offers up a password field to login with.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">'Enter password' step</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image {"linkDestination":"custom"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><a href="https://users-content.uxcel.com/2318dc26-2162-42fc-92ba-cd4266e7ed81/briefs/reset-password-mobile-app-flow-4235-1721158883902.png" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener"><img src="https://users-content.uxcel.com/2318dc26-2162-42fc-92ba-cd4266e7ed81/briefs/reset-password-mobile-app-flow-4235-1721158883902.png" alt=""/></a></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The forgot password link is placed beneath the password input, as chunking items of a similar nature together makes them easier for users to locate.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">'Reset password' step</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image {"linkDestination":"custom"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><a href="https://users-content.uxcel.com/2318dc26-2162-42fc-92ba-cd4266e7ed81/briefs/reset-password-mobile-app-flow-0632-1721158883903.png" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener"><img src="https://users-content.uxcel.com/2318dc26-2162-42fc-92ba-cd4266e7ed81/briefs/reset-password-mobile-app-flow-0632-1721158883903.png" alt=""/></a></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>On clicking the ‘Forgot password?’ link, the system opens a ‘Reset Password’ modal.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The ‘email address’ field is auto-populated with the address the user entered on the initial ‘enter email’ step, with the field auto-focused so that the keyboard is immediately accessible. This is so the user doesn't have to re-enter their email address.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The microcopy on the CTA relates to the modal title and the modal body copy, so that the user easily makes the connection and has an understanding of what will happen when they click the button.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Upon submitting, the user is presented with next steps. There is an animation that plays just once, which shows an envelope opening to reveal its contents. There is also microcopy to support the animation which tells the user what they need to do next.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image {"linkDestination":"custom"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><a href="https://users-content.uxcel.com/2318dc26-2162-42fc-92ba-cd4266e7ed81/briefs/reset-password-mobile-app-flow-3524-1721158883903.png" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener"><img src="https://users-content.uxcel.com/2318dc26-2162-42fc-92ba-cd4266e7ed81/briefs/reset-password-mobile-app-flow-3524-1721158883903.png" alt=""/></a></figure>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The email that the user receives includes:</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Clear branding to show who the email was from.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Body copy is written in short snappy sentences so that the user can quickly parse the information.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>The microcopy of ‘Reset password’ on the CTA makes it clear what will happen when the user clicks the button.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">New password entry on app launch</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image {"linkDestination":"custom"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><a href="https://users-content.uxcel.com/2318dc26-2162-42fc-92ba-cd4266e7ed81/briefs/reset-password-mobile-app-flow-1833-1721158883903.png" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener"><img src="https://users-content.uxcel.com/2318dc26-2162-42fc-92ba-cd4266e7ed81/briefs/reset-password-mobile-app-flow-1833-1721158883903.png" alt=""/></a></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In a perfect world, when clicking on the button from within the email, the app is opened, and the user is immediately presented with an ‘Enter new password’ modal with the field auto-focused so they have immediate access to the keyboard.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>I omitted the common ‘confirm new password’ option, as on mobile this presents a lot of usability issues for people who use password managers, as there’s often no way to retrieve and paste in the password into the second field.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Beneath the password field are the prerequisites that the system needs for a password before the user can continue. I designed these as greyed out ticks to show that they haven’t been achieved initially.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>As the user is typing and meets password prerequisites, the associated grey ticks turn green to show that the user has ‘succeeded.’</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Users can only trigger red crosses next to the password prerequisites if they press the ‘update password’ button before prerequisites have been met. I didn’t use the ‘disabled’ state for the update password button here to add another layer of validation checking.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"linkDestination":"custom"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><a href="https://users-content.uxcel.com/2318dc26-2162-42fc-92ba-cd4266e7ed81/briefs/reset-password-mobile-app-flow-5904-1721158883903.png" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener"><img src="https://users-content.uxcel.com/2318dc26-2162-42fc-92ba-cd4266e7ed81/briefs/reset-password-mobile-app-flow-5904-1721158883903.png" alt=""/></a></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Upon submission of the new password:</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>The system may ask the user to save their password depending on their OS. In this example, the iOS ‘save to keychain’ modal is shown.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>After accepting or declining the saved password the user could automatically be logged in, which would take them to the ‘logged in’ homepage of the app. In the flow I’ve designed, I’ve included the ‘password changed’ email in the flow. This email, which the user doesn’t need to be directed to, includes a clear CTA to login to the app.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>The user can then login to the app with their saved credentials or by entering the password into app.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->
