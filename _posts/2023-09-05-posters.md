<h2 class="wp-block-heading has-text-align-center has-white-color has-pale-pink-background-color has-text-color has-background">EXERCISE:&nbsp;Catalyst for Change</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"fontSize":"medium"} -->
<p class="has-medium-font-size">A graphic design solution can be a catalyst for change. If you had a chance to raise your voice in protest through a poster, what would you protest? War? Child abuse? Pollution? What would you promote? Freedom? Clean water? To whom would you appeal? Select one cause that you deem important.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3,"fontSize":"medium"} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading has-medium-font-size">Start with a blank sheet of paper and complete the following:
</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item {"fontSize":"medium"} -->
<li class="has-medium-font-size">Sketch or write as many objects as you can think of having similarities to your subject. If you’re having difficulty, use <strong>attribute listing</strong> to help you or treat this as a kind of Rorschach inkblot test, where you sketch the first thing that comes to mind when asking yourself, “What might this remind me of or be similar to?”</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item {"fontSize":"medium"} -->
<li class="has-medium-font-size">Sketch visual metaphors, at least two, for your cause.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"fontSize":"medium"} -->
<p class="has-medium-font-size"><strong>Attribute listing</strong> involves identifying the key design features of the potential product, service or strategy that you want to develop.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3,"className":"remove-bottom-margin","backgroundColor":"luminous-vivid-amber","textColor":"black"} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading remove-bottom-margin has-black-color has-luminous-vivid-amber-background-color has-text-color has-background">Attribute Listing: </h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":"remove-top-margin","backgroundColor":"luminous-vivid-amber"} -->
<p class="remove-top-margin has-luminous-vivid-amber-background-color has-background">In morphological analysis, you formalize these features in a matrix. Then, you choose interesting combinations of items or select items at random from each column.&nbsp;[1]</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"fontSize":"small"} -->
<p class="has-small-font-size">[1] Zwicky, F. (1969). ‘Discovery, Invention, Research Through the Morphological Approach,’ London: Macmillan.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"backgroundColor":"pale-pink","textColor":"white"} -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading has-white-color has-pale-pink-background-color has-text-color has-background">POSTER DESIGN FOR A SOCIAL OR POLITICAL CAUSE</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Step 1</strong>
</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:list {"ordered":true} -->
<ol class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item {"fontSize":"medium"} -->
<li class="has-medium-font-size">Select a social or political cause. Gather information about it.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item {"fontSize":"medium"} -->
<li class="has-medium-font-size">Find related visuals to use as references.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item {"fontSize":"medium"} -->
<li class="has-medium-font-size">Write a design brief. Define the purpose and function of the poster, the audience, and the information to be communicated.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item {"fontSize":"medium"} -->
<li class="has-medium-font-size">Generate a few design concepts. Concentrate your conceptual thinking on finding a way to prompt people to think about the cause. Select and refine one concept.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ol>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:separator {"opacity":"css"} -->
<hr class="wp-block-separator has-css-opacity"/>
<!-- /wp:separator -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Step 2</strong></h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:list {"ordered":true,"fontSize":"medium"} -->
<ol class="wp-block-list has-medium-font-size"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Determine whether the poster should be visually or type-driven.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Your poster should be able to grab the attention of people walking by.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>The poster should include the social cause’s web address and phone number so that people can take action.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Determine at least three different ways your concept could be visualized. (sketches).</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ol>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:separator {"opacity":"css"} -->
<hr class="wp-block-separator has-css-opacity"/>
<!-- /wp:separator -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Step 3</strong>
</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:list {"ordered":true,"fontSize":"medium"} -->
<ol class="wp-block-list has-medium-font-size"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Produce at least<strong> two roughs</strong> <strong>(In Illustrator/Photoshop)</strong> before starting the final comp.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Be sure to establish a visual hierarchy.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>The poster can be in either a vertical or a horizontal format.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ol>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:separator {"opacity":"css"} -->
<hr class="wp-block-separator has-css-opacity"/>
<!-- /wp:separator -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Step 4 </h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:list {"ordered":true,"fontSize":"medium"} -->
<ol class="wp-block-list has-medium-font-size"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Refine the roughs. Create one Final comp.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>The size, shape, and proportion should be dictated by your strategy, design concept, and where the poster will be seen (environment).</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Use two main colors. Use supplementary colors when you feel it's appropriate.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ol>
