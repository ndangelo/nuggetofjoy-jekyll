<h2>Redesigning the Swedish Tourism Site</h2>
<p>A step-by-step project guide: research how visitors plan a trip, map the user journey, use the existing UI/UX Style Guide template, design responsive website screens, and build and test an interactive prototype.</p>

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">The Project</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Your challenge is to redesign the Swedish tourism website to help people discover Sweden and plan a visit. Make the experience easier to navigate, more informative, and more useful to a clearly defined audience. Do not try to redesign every page. Identify one meaningful user problem and build a connected responsive website experience that addresses it.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:html -->

<div class="sweden-deliverable"><span>04</span><h3>Interactive Prototype</h3><p>Connect the visitor journey and refine it with Figma Make.</p></div>
<div class="sweden-deliverable"><span>05</span><h3>User Testing</h3><p>Test, revise, and test again with visitors.</p></div>

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Client Brief: Swedish Tourism</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Objective</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Improve the tourism website experience by helping prospective visitors explore Sweden, find destinations and experiences, access practical travel information, and plan a trip. Your redesign should address a specific pain point supported by research and testing.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Target audiences</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>First-time visitor:</strong> wants to understand what Sweden offers and find a destination that fits their time, interests, and budget.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Culture-focused traveler:</strong> wants to discover Swedish design, food, museums, and local cultural experiences.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Family travelers:</strong> want practical information about activities, transport, seasons, and planning a family-friendly visit.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:html -->
<div class="sweden-callout"><strong>Choose one primary audience.</strong> These are possible audience types for your research, not supplied personas or research findings. Validate your assumptions and make design decisions for one main user and one clearly defined task.</div>
<!-- /wp:html -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Requirements to consider</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:list {"className":"wp-block-list"} -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Clear navigation for destinations, activities, and trip planning.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Useful ways to discover places by interest, region, season, or travel style.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Destination and experience information that supports decisions.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Practical travel information, such as getting around, weather/season, and accessibility where relevant.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Clear calls to action to save, compare, or plan a trip.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Responsive layouts that work on desktop and mobile.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>You do not have to add every feature. Use research to choose the most important visitor problem and keep your prototype focused.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">01 — Research the Existing Experience</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Before redesigning the Swedish tourism site, examine the current website and ask prospective travelers how they research destinations, compare experiences, and plan a visit. Observe where information is hard to find, where choices feel unclear, or what questions remain unanswered.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list {"className":"wp-block-list"} -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Identify one visitor task and one recurring point of friction.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Record what people currently do and where they struggle.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Review tourism websites for other destinations to compare navigation, content structure, and planning tools.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Write a short problem statement and a “How might we…” question.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:html -->
<div class="sweden-callout"><strong>Example problem statement:</strong> “First-time visitors are interested in Sweden but struggle to narrow a broad set of destinations and experiences into a realistic trip.” This is only an example. Use your own observations to define your actual problem.</div>
<!-- /wp:html -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">02 — Create Your User Flow in FigJam</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Before designing screens, choose one task for your visitor. Map the starting point, the actions and decisions along the way, and what success looks like. Your flow should describe the logic of the experience—not just show a row of finished app screens.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:html -->
<div class="sweden-callout"><strong>Your user flow should show</strong><ul><li>The starting point and the user's goal.</li><li>The sequence of actions and screens.</li><li>Decisions and alternative paths.</li><li>Relevant error, empty, or recovery paths.</li><li>The successful completion of the task.</li></ul></div>
<!-- /wp:html -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Use the course template.</strong> Open the AMDE 477 FigJam User Flow Template, duplicate it, and adapt the structure to your chosen visitor task. Keep the flow focused and make the labels easy to follow.</p>

<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:html -->

<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Establish a consistent hierarchy for headings, body text, labels, and supporting text. Choose spacing values and apply them consistently across your screens.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:html -->
<figure class="sweden-reference sweden-template-shot"><img src="https://www.figma.com/api/mcp/asset/109fd972-a72f-404c-900c-9e0458a899bc.png" alt="Actual Student UI/UX Style Guide template section for typography hierarchy and spacing" loading="lazy"><figcaption><strong>Actual course template — Typography &amp; Spacing.</strong> Use this existing section to document your type scale and spacing rules.</figcaption></figure>

<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->

Include accessibility considerations such as contrast, legible text, clear labels, and visible focus.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">04 — Design the Flat Screens in Figma Design</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Use your user flow and style guide to design the key screens in Figma Design first. Keep the screens organized and name each frame clearly. You should be able to see the visual design before you ask Figma Make to help create the interactive experience.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Your screen set should include the responsive website pages needed for your chosen flow. Use these page types as a starting point and select the ones that support your user journey:</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">06 — Test, Revise, and Test Again</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Test your early wireframes or prototype with five people. Give each person a realistic task without explaining every step. Observe where they hesitate, make mistakes, or misunderstand a control. Ask what they would improve and request an overall rating using the same scale for everyone.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list {"className":"wp-block-list"} -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Round 1: Test with five people and record observations, comments, and ratings.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Identify the most important usability issues; prioritize changes that prevent task completion or cause confusion.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Revise your flow, screens, and prototype based on the evidence.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Round 2: Test the revised design with five people. Use the same tasks and rating scale so you can compare results.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Report what changed and whether the second round improved the experience. If it did not, explain what you would revise next.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->
