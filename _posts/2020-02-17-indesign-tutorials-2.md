<h3>How to Design a Classic Book Cover With a Modern Twist!</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Redesigning covers for classic and beloved novels is big business in publishing. A well-tuned redesign can help a book reach new audiences across different generations and demographics.&nbsp;</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:more -->
<!--more-->
<!-- /wp:more -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In this tutorial, we’ll dip a toe into the world of classic covers and create a redesigned cover for&nbsp;<em>Moby Dick</em>&nbsp;that’s modern, stylish and surprisingly easy to recreate in Adobe InDesign and Adobe Illustrator.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>This is a great tutorial for beginners to InDesign looking to learn more about book cover design and publishing design.&nbsp;</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2>1.&nbsp;What Are Redesigned Book Covers?</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Before we delve into designing our cover for&nbsp;<em>Moby Dick</em>, let’s take a moment to consider what a redesigned cover is and why publishers invest money in commissioning redesigns.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Some novels are long-standing classics and have remained well-loved favorites for centuries or decades. Think of Jane Austen’s&nbsp;<em>Pride &amp; Prejudice</em>, Emily Brontë’s&nbsp;<em>Wuthering Heights</em>, F. Scott Fitzgerald’s<em>&nbsp;The Great Gatsby</em>, or Harper Lee’s&nbsp;<em>To Kill a Mockingbird</em>. These novels have earned a firm place in the annals of literature, but sometimes younger generations either won’t have encountered these classic stories, or have the perception that they are stuffy, outdated or difficult to read.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Publishers introduce redesigned covers for classic novels periodically to inject them with fresh marketing potential and entice a new demographic of readers.&nbsp;</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In many ways, redesigning covers for classic books is one of the most interesting, but also most difficult, processes a cover designer will encounter in their career. Their covers must do justice to the original spirit and themes of the book, while giving it a design that’s got a distinctly modern appeal.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In this tutorial we’ll look at giving Herman Melville’s&nbsp;<em>Moby Dick or “The Whale”</em>&nbsp;a modern makeover. I’ve tried to recreate the mood and theme of the book, with a silhouette of the white sperm whale from the story.&nbsp;</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center"} -->
<div class="wp-block-image"><figure class="aligncenter"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/Full-cover-3.jpg" alt="finished cover"/></figure></div>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>You’ll learn how to set up a paperback version of the cover in&nbsp;<strong>Adobe InDesign</strong>&nbsp;and create a collage effect with the help of&nbsp;<strong>Adobe Illustrator</strong>. We’ll bring in a bit of vintage-style typography too, to give the cover an appropriately 19th Century nod.&nbsp;</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>This design style can be adapted to all sorts of covers—just switch up the silhouette and inside image, as in this cover design for&nbsp;<em>War &amp; Peace</em>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/War-and-peace-mock-up-1.png" alt="war and peace redesign"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Ready to get redesigning? Great! Let’s go...</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2>2.&nbsp;Set Up the Cover Template in Adobe InDesign</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3>Step 1</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Open up&nbsp;<strong>Adobe InDesign.&nbsp;</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Go to&nbsp;<strong>File &gt; New &gt; Document</strong>, and set the Intent of the document to&nbsp;<strong>Print</strong>. Increase the&nbsp;<strong>Number of Pages</strong>&nbsp;to&nbsp;<strong>2</strong>&nbsp;and deselect&nbsp;<strong>Facing Pages</strong>. From the&nbsp;<strong>Page Size</strong>&nbsp;menu, choose&nbsp;<strong>Custom</strong>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/1.1-1.png" alt="custom size"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Name the custom size&nbsp;<strong>Paperback Book</strong>, and set the&nbsp;<strong>Width</strong>&nbsp;to&nbsp;<strong>132 mm</strong>&nbsp;and&nbsp;<strong>Height</strong>&nbsp;to&nbsp;<strong>197 mm</strong>. Click&nbsp;<strong>Add</strong>&nbsp;and then&nbsp;<strong>OK</strong>&nbsp;to return to the&nbsp;<strong>New Document</strong>&nbsp;window.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/1.1.2-1.png" alt="custom page size"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3>Step 2</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Set the&nbsp;<strong>Margins</strong>&nbsp;on all sides to&nbsp;<strong>11 mm</strong>&nbsp;and the&nbsp;<strong>Bleed</strong>&nbsp;to&nbsp;<strong>5 mm</strong>. Click&nbsp;<strong>OK</strong>&nbsp;to create the new document.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/1.2-1.png" alt="new document window"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/1.2.1-1.png" alt="new document"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We’ll work on the front cover design on&nbsp;<strong>Page 1</strong>—it’s always much easier to work on the front cover design alone at first. Then, a bit later, we’ll expand Page 2 of the document into the full cover, complete with spine and reverse, with the help of the Page Tool.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3>Step 3</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>With the rulers visible<strong>&nbsp;(View &gt; Show Rulers)&nbsp;</strong>click and drag a guide out from the left-hand ruler and drop it onto&nbsp;<strong>Page 1</strong>&nbsp;at&nbsp;<strong>X position 66 mm</strong>, which marks the center point of the page.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/1.32.png" alt="controls panel"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/1.3.1-1.png" alt="center point of cover"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2>3.&nbsp;Create a Seaworthy Backdrop</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>To recreate the murky, mysterious ocean setting of<em>&nbsp;Moby Dick</em>, we can give the whole cover a sea-green backdrop. Adding a gradient to the swatch will give the cover a bit more depth and interest.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3>Step 1</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Take the&nbsp;<strong>Rectangle Tool (M)</strong>&nbsp;and drag onto&nbsp;<strong>Page 1</strong>, across the whole page, extending up to the edges of the bleed on the top, right and bottom edges of the page. On the left edge, just extend to the edge of the page, not all the way to the bleed.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/3.1-1.png" alt="rectangle tool"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3>Step 2</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Expand or open the&nbsp;<strong>Swatches</strong>&nbsp;panel&nbsp;<strong>(Window &gt; Color &gt; Swatches).&nbsp;</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>From the panel’s drop-down menu, choose&nbsp;<strong>New Gradient Swatch.</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/3.2-2.png" alt="new gradient swatch"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Name the swatch&nbsp;<strong>Green Gradient</strong>&nbsp;and set the&nbsp;<strong>Type</strong>&nbsp;to&nbsp;<strong>Radial</strong>.&nbsp;</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Move down to the&nbsp;<strong>Gradient Ramp</strong>&nbsp;and click on the left-hand stop to be able to edit it. Choose&nbsp;<strong>CMYK</strong>&nbsp;for the&nbsp;<strong>Stop Color</strong>&nbsp;and set the levels to&nbsp;<strong>C=89 M=49 Y=62 K=62.</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/3.2.1-1.png" alt="green gradient"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Click on the right-hand stop and adjust the&nbsp;<strong>Stop Color</strong>&nbsp;to&nbsp;<strong>Swatches</strong>. Choose&nbsp;<strong>[Black]</strong>&nbsp;from the list below. Click&nbsp;<strong>OK</strong>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/3.2.2-1.png" alt="green gradient"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3>Step 3</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Apply the&nbsp;<strong>Green Gradient&nbsp;</strong>swatch to the&nbsp;<strong>Fill Color</strong>&nbsp;of the rectangle shape.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/3.3-1.png" alt="green gradient applied to shape"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>This will apply a lovely subtle gradient to the background of our front cover.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/3.3.1-1.png" alt="completed backdrop"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2>4.&nbsp;Create a Whale Silhouette</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3>Step 1</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Expand or open the&nbsp;<strong>Layers</strong>&nbsp;panel&nbsp;<strong>(Window &gt; Layers)</strong>&nbsp;and double-click on the default&nbsp;<strong><em>Layer 1</em></strong>&nbsp;name. Rename the layer as<strong><em>&nbsp;Background Color&nbsp;</em></strong>and click<strong>&nbsp;OK</strong>.&nbsp;</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/4.1-1.png" alt="layer options"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Click on the<strong>&nbsp;Create New Layer</strong>&nbsp;button at the bottom right of the&nbsp;<strong>Layers</strong>&nbsp;panel to create a new layer. Double-click, and rename the layer as&nbsp;<strong><em>Graphics</em></strong>.&nbsp;</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/4.1.1-2.png" alt="layer options"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Lock the bottom layer,&nbsp;<strong><em>Background Color</em></strong>, by clicking in the blank space to the right of the eye icon, next to the name of the layer in the panel.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/4.1.2-1.png" alt="layers panel"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3>Step 2</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Now we’re ready to start working on our whale graphic!</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>File &gt; Save As</strong>&nbsp;your&nbsp;<strong>InDesign</strong>&nbsp;document, and minimize InDesign. Open up<strong>&nbsp;Adobe Illustrator</strong>&nbsp;and create a new document.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>To make our collage-style whale, we need to first create a basic vector silhouette of the whale, and then, a bit later, we’ll fill it with a photo image.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/4.22.png" alt="completed whale"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>You can either draw the whale manually using the&nbsp;<strong>Pen Tool (P)</strong>&nbsp;in&nbsp;<strong>Illustrator</strong>, using photos of whales as a guide, or you can download a ready-made&nbsp;<a href="https://image-control-storage.s3.amazonaws.com/Sperm_whale_by_Rones.svg" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener" aria-label="vector image of a sperm whale (opens in a new tab)">vector image of a sperm whale</a>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>If you choose the latter, you’ll have to remove the stroke color of the sperm whale, get rid of any details and create a very simple black-filled silhouette of the whale shape, as in the example below.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/4.2.1-1.png" alt="vector in illustrator"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3>Step 3</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Making sure your whale silhouette has only a simple black fill and no stroke color, select it, and then go up to&nbsp;<strong>Edit &gt; Copy</strong>&nbsp;in Illustrator.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Return to your&nbsp;<strong>InDesign</strong>&nbsp;document and&nbsp;<strong>Edit &gt; Paste</strong>&nbsp;the whale onto&nbsp;<strong>Page 1</strong>&nbsp;of your document. Hold&nbsp;<strong>Shift</strong>&nbsp;to adjust the scale of the pasted vector. Expand the whale so that its head fills the center of the page, as shown below, and so that the body extends past the left edge of the page, and past the edge of the pasteboard if needed. We want the whale to extend across the whole cover, including the spine and the reverse of the cover.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/4.32.png" alt="pasted vector"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>From the&nbsp;<strong>Swatches</strong>&nbsp;panel&nbsp;<strong>(Window &gt; Color &gt; Swatches)</strong>, set the&nbsp;<strong>Fill Color</strong>&nbsp;of the vector to&nbsp;<strong>[None]</strong>&nbsp;to get rid of theblack&nbsp;fill.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/4.3.12.png" alt="whale frame"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3>Step 4</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a href="https://image-control-storage.s3.amazonaws.com/blue-and-white-ocean-756856.jpg" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener" aria-label="Download sea image and return to your InDesign document. (opens in a new tab)">Download sea image and return to your InDesign document.</a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/4.4-1.jpg" alt="sea foam photo"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>With the whale vector selected, go to&nbsp;<strong>File &gt; Place&nbsp;</strong>and choose the sea foam image. Click&nbsp;<strong>Open</strong>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Click on the&nbsp;<strong>Fill Frame Proportionally</strong>&nbsp;button in the top control panel to size the image to the frame. Double-click inside the whale to manually adjust the scale of the image, holding&nbsp;<strong>Shift</strong>&nbsp;while you do so, to make sure you’re happy with the effect. Make sure the image extends across the whole of the whale, right up to the edge of its tail.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/4.4.1-1.jpg" alt="whale head"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3>Step 5</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Select the whale and&nbsp;<strong>Edit &gt; Copy, Edit &gt; Paste</strong>. Hold down&nbsp;<strong>Shift</strong>&nbsp;and reduce the whale in size until it’s just shy of the width of the page in length.&nbsp;<strong>Edit &gt; Copy</strong>&nbsp;this smaller whale, so you keep a copy on your clipboard.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Control-Click (Mac) or Right-Click (Windows) &gt; Transform &gt; Flip Horizontal</strong>. Position this smaller whale at the top of the page, and allow it to cross over the top edge, so it doesn’t look too perfectly placed. You can rotate it a little too, so it doesn’t look quite as straight.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/4.5-1.jpg" alt="flip horizontal"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/4.5.1-1.jpg" alt="whale flipped"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Go to&nbsp;<strong>Edit &gt; Paste</strong>&nbsp;to paste a copy of the smaller whale again. Position at the bottom-left of the page, just allowing the tail to appear on the page itself, as shown below.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/4.5.2-1.jpg" alt="pasted whale"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2>5.&nbsp;Add Vintage Typography</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3>Step 1</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Return to the&nbsp;<strong>Layers</strong>&nbsp;panel and create a new layer. Rename it&nbsp;<strong><em>Typography</em></strong>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/5.1-2.png" alt="layer options"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Lock the&nbsp;<strong><em>Graphics</em></strong>&nbsp;layer and click on the&nbsp;<strong><em>Typography</em></strong>&nbsp;layer to activate it.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/5.1.1-1.png" alt="layers panel"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Download and install these two (free!) fonts:</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul><li><a href="https://www.fontsquirrel.com/fonts/leander" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Leander</a></li><li><a href="http://www.dafont.com/oranienbaum.font" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Oranienbaum</a></li></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>You’ll also need&nbsp;<strong>Bodoni Std</strong>, but this should be installed already in your InDesign font list. If it’s not, a great free alternative is&nbsp;<a href="http://www.fontsquirrel.com/fonts/BodoniXT" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">BodoniXT</a>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3>Step 2</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Return to the&nbsp;<strong>Swatches</strong>&nbsp;panel and click on the&nbsp;<strong>New Swatch</strong>&nbsp;button at the bottom-right corner of the panel.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Double-click on the new swatch to open up the&nbsp;<strong>Swatch Options</strong>&nbsp;window. Rename the swatch as&nbsp;<strong>Mint</strong>, and set the&nbsp;<strong>Color Type</strong>&nbsp;to&nbsp;<strong>Process</strong>&nbsp;and&nbsp;<strong>Color Mode</strong>&nbsp;to&nbsp;<strong>CMYK</strong>. Set the levels to&nbsp;<strong>C=54 M=9 Y=35 K=0.</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Click&nbsp;<strong>OK</strong>&nbsp;to add the revised swatch to the&nbsp;<strong>Swatches</strong>&nbsp;panel.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/5.2-1.png" alt="mint swatch"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3>Step 3</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Select the&nbsp;<strong>Type Tool (T)</strong>&nbsp;and drag onto the top left of&nbsp;<strong>Page 1</strong>&nbsp;to create a text frame. Rest the left edge of the frame against the left-hand margin.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Type&nbsp;<em>'Moby-Dick’</em>&nbsp;and, from either the&nbsp;<strong>Character Formatting Controls</strong>&nbsp;panel running along the top of the workspace or the&nbsp;<strong>Character</strong>&nbsp;panel&nbsp;<strong>(Window &gt; Type &amp; Tables &gt; Character)</strong>, set the&nbsp;<strong>Font</strong>&nbsp;to&nbsp;<strong>Oranienbaum, Size 55 pt.</strong>&nbsp;Reduce the&nbsp;<strong>Tracking</strong>&nbsp;(space between all letters) to&nbsp;<strong>-20</strong>, and set the text to uppercase by clicking on the&nbsp;<strong>All Caps</strong>&nbsp;button in the&nbsp;<strong>Controls</strong>&nbsp;panel.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Adjust the<strong>&nbsp;Font Color</strong>&nbsp;to&nbsp;<strong>Mint</strong>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/5.3-1.png" alt="moby-dick title"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3>Step 4</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Return to the&nbsp;<strong>Swatches</strong>&nbsp;panel and create another new CMYK swatch, this time with the values&nbsp;<strong>C=10 M=0 Y=13 K=0</strong>. Rename the swatch as&nbsp;<strong>Pale Mint.</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/5.4-1.png" alt="pale mint"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Move down the page, and zoom into the right-hand edge, just where the whale’s mouth should be. Create a new smaller text frame and type in&nbsp;<em>‘or’.&nbsp;</em></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Set the&nbsp;<strong>Font</strong>&nbsp;to&nbsp;<strong>Bodoni Std, Italic, Size 22 pt</strong>, and increase the&nbsp;<strong>Tracking</strong>&nbsp;to&nbsp;<strong>50</strong>. From the&nbsp;<strong>Paragraph Formatting Controls</strong>&nbsp;panel at the top of the workspace, adjust the text to&nbsp;<strong>Align Right.&nbsp;</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Set the&nbsp;<strong>Font Color</strong>&nbsp;to&nbsp;<strong>Pale Mint.</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/5.4.1-1.png" alt="or text"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3>Step 5</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Create another new swatch, this time called&nbsp;<strong>Pale Lime</strong>, and with the values&nbsp;<strong>C=9 M=4 Y=21 K=0.</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/5.5-1.png" alt="pale lime"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Create a new text frame and position it below the<em>&nbsp;‘or’</em>&nbsp;text frame, to the right side of the page. Type in&nbsp;<em>‘“The Whale”’&nbsp;</em>and set the&nbsp;<strong>Font</strong>&nbsp;to&nbsp;<strong>Oranienbaum</strong>,&nbsp;<strong>Size 40 pt, Tracking -20</strong>&nbsp;and&nbsp;<strong>All Caps</strong>. Set the text to&nbsp;<strong>Align Right.</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Pull out&nbsp;<em>‘The Whale’&nbsp;</em>in the&nbsp;<strong>Mint</strong>&nbsp;swatch, and the apostrophes in the&nbsp;<strong>Pale Lime</strong>&nbsp;swatch.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/5.5.1-1.png" alt="the whale title"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3>Step 6</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Create a final new text frame and position in the bottom-left corner of the page, resting against the margins.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Type in&nbsp;<em>‘Herman Melville’&nbsp;</em>and set the&nbsp;<strong>Font</strong>&nbsp;to&nbsp;<strong>Leander, Size 20 pt, Tracking 70</strong>, and&nbsp;<strong>Font Color</strong>&nbsp;to&nbsp;<strong>Pale Lime.</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/5.6-1.png" alt="author name"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>And there we have it! Your front cover design is complete, and it’s looking great!</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/5.6.1-1.jpg" alt="front cover design"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In the final part of the tutorial, we’ll look at how we can expand the design into a full cover, with spine and reverse cover. Read on to find out how...</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2>6.&nbsp;Expand Your Design Into a Full Cover</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3>Step 1</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Unlock all the layers in the&nbsp;<strong>Layers</strong>&nbsp;panel, and then drag across&nbsp;<strong>Page 1</strong>&nbsp;to select everything on the page.&nbsp;<strong>Edit &gt; Copy</strong>, and scroll down to&nbsp;<strong>Page 2</strong>&nbsp;of your document.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Edit &gt; Paste in Place</strong>&nbsp;to make a copy of the front cover on the page.&nbsp;</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/6.16.jpg" alt="pasted page"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Now we need to work out the width of the full length of the cover. This will depend on the width of the spine, which in turn will depend on the number of pages inside the book. You can find calculators online for working out the width of a spine depending on the page number and paper weight (gsm), such as the&nbsp;<a href="http://www.printondemand-worldwide.com/resources/spinecalculator" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Print on Demand calculator</a>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Using this, I’ve worked out that to accommodate&nbsp;1,456 pages (the length of&nbsp;<em>Moby Dick</em>—yup, it’s a long one!) printed on white offset 80 gsm paper, we will need to have a&nbsp;<strong>73.7 mm</strong>&nbsp;spine width.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The equation for the full width of the cover is this:</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Front Cover Width (x) + Spine Width (y) + Back Cover Width (x)&nbsp;</strong><strong>= Total Width (x + x + y)</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>So, for our&nbsp;<em>Moby Dick</em>&nbsp;cover it’s going to be...</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>132 mm + 73.7 mm + 132 mm =&nbsp;<strong>337.7 mm Total Width</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3>Step 2</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Select the&nbsp;<strong>Page Tool (Shift + P)&nbsp;</strong>and, holding down&nbsp;<strong>Option (Windows)&nbsp;</strong>or&nbsp;<strong>Alt (Mac)</strong>, drag the left-hand edge of the page out to the left, increasing the page width to the full&nbsp;<strong>337.7 mm</strong>. When you let go, the page width will remain at its new width.&nbsp;</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/6.2-1.png" alt="page tool"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>If you’re struggling to get the width exactly right as you drag, you can simply type in the correct width in the box at the top-left corner of the workspace.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/6.2.1-1.png" alt="controls panel"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3>Step 3</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>From the left-hand ruler, pull out a guide to&nbsp;<strong>X position 137 mm</strong>, to mark out the left edge of the spine.&nbsp;</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/6.3-1.png" alt="guide"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Pull out a second guide to&nbsp;<strong>168.85 mm</strong>, which marks out the center point of the spine.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/6.3.1-1.png" alt="guide"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Pull out a third guide to&nbsp;<strong>68.5 mm</strong>, to mark out the center of the back cover.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/6.3.2-1.png" alt="guide"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Finally, pull out a guide to&nbsp;<strong>121 mm</strong>, which marks out the right-hand margin of the back cover.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/6.3.3-1.png" alt="guide"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3>Step 4</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Return to the&nbsp;<strong>Layers</strong>&nbsp;panel and lock all layers except the&nbsp;<strong><em>Background Color</em></strong>&nbsp;layer.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>From the&nbsp;<strong>Swatches</strong>&nbsp;panel, click on the&nbsp;<strong>Green Gradient&nbsp;</strong>swatch to select it, and then click on the&nbsp;<strong>New Swatch</strong>&nbsp;button to duplicate it.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Double-click on the duplicate swatch to open up the&nbsp;<strong>Swatch Options</strong>&nbsp;window.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Rename the swatch&nbsp;<strong>Green Gradient Spine</strong>. Keep everything as it is, and just move the right-hand stop on the&nbsp;<strong>Gradient Ramp&nbsp;</strong>to the left, until you reach about&nbsp;<strong>54%</strong>. Click&nbsp;<strong>OK</strong>&nbsp;to save your edits.&nbsp;</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/6.4-1.png" alt="gradient swatch"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Use the&nbsp;<strong>Rectangle Tool (M)&nbsp;</strong>to create a shape that extends across the spine, and up to the bleed on the top and bottom (<strong>73.7 mm</strong>&nbsp;in&nbsp;<strong>Width</strong>). Set the&nbsp;<strong>Fill Color</strong>&nbsp;to&nbsp;<strong>Gradient Green Spine</strong>. The gradient on this is a bit tighter, so the lighter tone will not extend too far away from the central part of the spine.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/6.4.1-1.png" alt="color on spine"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Create a second shape using the&nbsp;<strong>Rectangle Tool (M)</strong>, extending it over the whole of the back cover, up to the left edge of the spine, and up to the bleed on the top, left and bottom edges of the page. Set the&nbsp;<strong>Fill</strong>&nbsp;to&nbsp;<strong>Green Gradient.</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/6.4.2-1.png" alt="background on back cover"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3>Step 5</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Return to the&nbsp;<strong>Layers</strong>&nbsp;panel and lock the<strong><em>&nbsp;Background Color</em></strong>&nbsp;layer. Unlock the next layer up,&nbsp;<strong><em>Graphics</em></strong>.&nbsp;</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Copy</strong>&nbsp;and&nbsp;<strong>Paste</strong>&nbsp;one of the smaller whale graphics a couple of times and get creative with placing them on the back cover and spine. Rotate them, just showing a touch of tail on the spine perhaps, or allow them to cross over onto the pasteboard to give the impression of a full pod of sperm whales.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/6.5-1.png" alt="pasted whales"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/6.5.1-1.png" alt="preview of cover"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3>Step 6</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Lock the&nbsp;<strong><em>Graphics</em></strong>&nbsp;layer and unlock the top layer,&nbsp;<strong><em>Typography</em></strong>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Zoom in on the spine and create a few rows of text frames using the&nbsp;<strong>Type Tool (T)</strong>. Type in the title, allowing one text frame for each line of the title, and mimic the typography formatting used on the front cover but at a smaller&nbsp;<strong>Font Size</strong>. Set all the text to&nbsp;<strong>Align Center&nbsp;</strong>and make sure the frames are perfectly centered along the central guide.&nbsp;</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/6.6-1.jpg" alt="spine text"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Add a blurb to the back cover, setting the text in two text frames, flushing text to the right, and the other to the left. Set the&nbsp;<strong>Font</strong>&nbsp;to&nbsp;<strong>Oranienbaum, Size 12 pt, Leading 16 pt</strong>. Use the swatch colors we created earlier to add a touch of color to the typography.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/6.6.1-1.jpg" alt="blurb on reverse"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2>7.&nbsp;Export Your Finished Cover</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Congratulations—the artwork for your&nbsp;<em>Moby Dick</em>&nbsp;cover is finished. Fantastic work!</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/713.jpg" alt="final cover"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>All that’s left to do now is to export it ready for printing.&nbsp;</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Of course, you can also export Page 1 of your document as an eBook cover (check the file format requirements of the eBook store before you upload it), but here I’ll walk you through the steps of exporting this as a print-ready file, which is suitable for sending to a professional book-printer.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3>Step 1</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Go to&nbsp;<strong>File &gt; Export</strong>. Choose&nbsp;<strong>Adobe PDF (Print)</strong>&nbsp;from the&nbsp;<strong>Format</strong>&nbsp;drop-down menu. Click&nbsp;<strong>Save</strong>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In the&nbsp;<strong>Export Adobe PDF</strong>&nbsp;window, choose&nbsp;<strong>[Press Quality]&nbsp;</strong>from the&nbsp;<strong>Adobe PDF Preset&nbsp;</strong>drop-down menu, and adjust the page range from&nbsp;<strong>All</strong>&nbsp;to&nbsp;<strong>Range: 2</strong>, just to export your full cover.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/7.12.jpg" alt="press quality"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>From the left-hand menu in the window, click on&nbsp;<strong>Marks and Bleeds</strong>. Check&nbsp;<strong>All Printer’s Marks</strong>&nbsp;and, under&nbsp;<strong>Bleed and Slug, Use Document Bleed Settings.&nbsp;</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/7.1.1-1.jpg" alt="marks and bleeds"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Click&nbsp;<strong>Export</strong>&nbsp;to create your print-ready cover.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Awesome!</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
