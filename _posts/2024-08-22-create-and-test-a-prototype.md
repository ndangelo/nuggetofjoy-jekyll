<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Today’s tutorial</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">1. Access the prototyping options</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>So far in this course, we’ve worked exclusively in the “Design” tab of the Properties panel (the area on the right of the window). Time to click the “Prototype” tab:&nbsp; &nbsp;</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><img src="https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/65130e79c72ae8812db3412e/6580ac5a38ed18874a93b01f_F101_D5_01.jpeg" alt="The prototype tab"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">2. Rename the Frames in your app flow</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Before creating any prototype, ensuring your Frames have distinctive names is a good idea. We’ve given our three screens descriptive titles: “Login Screen,” “Photo Stream,” and “Photo Post.” To rename a Frame, you must double-click its label at the top-left-hand corner of each Frame, type the new name, and hit Enter. You can also rename Frames in the Layers panel on the left.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Good naming conventions are especially important in larger projects—just imagine trying to build a 50-screen prototype if all of your Frames are named some variation of “iPhone SE - Copy 42”!</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:video -->
<figure class="wp-block-video"><video controls loop muted preload="none" src="https://storage.designlab.com/email-courses/figma-101/2023/F101_D5_02.mp4"></video></figure>
<!-- /wp:video -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">3. Set a starting Frame</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Prototyping in Figma works by creating links between Frames and elements within Frames. When we present the prototype, the user can click on linked elements to move around the app.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>First, we must establish the screen where the user experience (UX) begins. Click on the “Login Screen” Frame you designed in the Layers panel, and you’ll then see the options change in the Prototype tab of the Properties panel on the right. Then click the “+” icon to the right of where it says “Flow starting point” in the Properties panel.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:video -->
<figure class="wp-block-video"><video controls loop muted preload="none" src="https://storage.designlab.com/email-courses/figma-101/2023/F101_D5_03.mp4"></video></figure>
<!-- /wp:video -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">&nbsp; &nbsp;</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">4. Create a connection</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>If we select Frames or specific elements within our Frames while the Prototype tab is open, we get an extra, circular handle on the right-hand edge of every object. This handle allows us to set which screen that object leads to when it is tapped.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><img src="https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/65130e79c72ae8812db3412e/6580b349fd5624b348a0ce43_a-signin.jpg" alt=""/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Let’s use this to create a connection. First, select the “Sign In” button in your Login Screen. Then click and hold on that circular handle, and drag an arrow over to the Photo Stream to create a connection. This means that when the user taps the “Sign In” button in our prototype, they will be taken to the “Photo Stream” screen.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:video -->
<figure class="wp-block-video"><video controls loop muted preload="none" src="https://storage.designlab.com/email-courses/figma-101/2023/F101_D5_05.mp4"></video></figure>
<!-- /wp:video -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Once you’ve successfully made a connection, a small pop-up will appear confirming how the interaction will work. Take a peek at the drop-down options here. If you’ve clicked away already, you can reappear this pop-up by clicking on the light blue connect arrow extending from the “Sign In” button to the Photo Stream Frame.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">5. Create another connection</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Let’s repeat the process by linking one of the photos in the Photo Stream to the Photo Post Frame. Simply click on the heron photo and drag the connection handle over to the Photo Post Frame.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:video -->
<figure class="wp-block-video"><video controls loop muted preload="none" src="https://storage.designlab.com/email-courses/figma-101/2023/F101_D5_06.mp4"></video></figure>
<!-- /wp:video -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">6. Create connections for the “back” button</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Repeat this process once more to create a connection from the “Home” icon we created in the nav bar to the Photo Stream page. This is pretty rudimentary functionality, but it will give your users a feel for how they will move around the app.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:video -->
<figure class="wp-block-video"><video controls loop muted preload="none" src="https://storage.designlab.com/email-courses/figma-101/2023/F101_D5_07.mp4"></video></figure>
<!-- /wp:video -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">7. Set up a “Profile” page</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We’ve got time today to create one extra screen. This allows us to practice moving between the Design and Prototype tabs in the Properties panel and get a feel for managing our prototype connections while adding to the app design.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Duplicate your Photo Post Frame by selecting that Frame and hitting&nbsp;⌘D&nbsp;(Mac) or&nbsp;CtrlD&nbsp;(PC). Did you notice that when you duplicated the Frame, the “home” button connection was also carried across to the new Frame? Any connections that originate from the duplicated Frame will also be copied over. This can save work but also create problems if the connections aren’t updated where necessary.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In this case, we want to keep the one connection from the “Home” button to the Photo Stream page. To remove a link between screens, simply click on the connection arrow and hit Delete on your keyboard.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">10. Test your prototype</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>You can launch your prototype by hitting the “Present” button at the top-right of the Figma interface. Do this now to check that all your connections are working as planned!</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Note that it’s possible to present your prototype in a device container in the latest version of Figma. While in the “Prototype” tab, select “iPhone SE” from the dropdown in the “Device” section. Then, click the “Present” button again! One of the advantages of using a simulation like this is that it gives a more accurate experience of the size and scale of screen elements.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:video -->
<figure class="wp-block-video"><video controls loop muted preload="none" src="https://storage.designlab.com/email-courses/figma-101/2023/F101_D5_11.mp4" playsinline></video></figure>

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">11. Share your prototype</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>To share your designs as prototypes, copy the link from your browser’s address bar while you’re in “Present” mode and send it via email or instant message. Then, get a friend or family member to try using the prototype.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Voilá, you’re doing user testing! Watch them use the prototype. Do they intuitively know where to click? What ideas does watching them give you for how to improve the design?</p>
