<h3>Teaching Art - Composition</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3>Finding the visual sweet spots&nbsp;</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center","id":56911,"width":389,"height":263,"sizeSlug":"large"} -->
<div class="wp-block-image"><figure class="aligncenter size-large is-resized"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/ruleofthirds2.jpg" alt="" class="wp-image-56911" width="389" height="263"/></figure></div>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Version 1. </strong>Middle? No. When designing the composition of a drawing or<br>painting you need to make it interesting to the viewer. Try not the place your focal point dead center. &nbsp;It's boring. Artist's no.1 no-no rule.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center","id":56913,"sizeSlug":"large"} -->
<div class="wp-block-image"><figure class="aligncenter size-large"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/ruleofthirds3-1.jpg" alt="" class="wp-image-56913"/></figure></div>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Version 2.</strong> A little better…&nbsp;Here change happens in the composition<br>when the focal point hits a "sweet spot" in the composition.<br>But it's not quite there - the horizon line is too close to the center of the paper.&nbsp;</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center","id":56914,"sizeSlug":"large"} -->
<div class="wp-block-image"><figure class="aligncenter size-large"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/ruleofthirds4.jpg" alt="" class="wp-image-56914"/></figure></div>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Version 3. </strong>The keeper! This composition offers a pleasing balance<br>while being more dramatic &amp; interesting then version 2. The horizon line 2/3rds high.&nbsp;&nbsp;</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Here's a couple things I said as students were drawing:&nbsp;</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul><li>DO&nbsp;overlap objects to add depth to your drawing.&nbsp;</li><li>DON'T place objects that almost touch or are "tickling"&nbsp;each others edges.&nbsp;</li><li>DO group objects&nbsp;creatively - have fun!</li><li>DON'T&nbsp;balance equally - this is boring.</li><li>THINK about where your horizon line is - should it be in the center of your paper?&nbsp;</li></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<p><strong>Two Ways to Look at Composition: Focus and Effects</strong>.<br>We can use compositions to guide the viewer's eye to a point or points we want them to look at. Focus.</p>
