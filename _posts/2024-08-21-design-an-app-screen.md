<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Today’s tutorial</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In the last class, we explored the interface and learned about a few of Figma’s core functions. Today, we’ll use these commands and more to build the entire first screen of our app!</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Over the next few days, we’ll create screen designs for a photo-sharing app. Feel free to pick whatever name you like—we’re choosing “filter” for ours. Let’s get started!</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">1. Open up your Figma file, and create the Frame for your app screen.</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>First, open up the same Figma file you were working on yesterday. (It should show up in tappearent” tab on the Figma home screen.)</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Like we did yesterday, hit F to select the Frame tool, and pick iPhone SE from the options on the right of the window. Then let’s, set a background color for this screen. While the new Frame is selected, look over e Properties panel on the right of the Figma window. Click the white square under where it says “Fill”, and choose a color. We’ve gone for #2F80ED, which is the shade of blue.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">2. Add a column grid</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>It’s important to work using a column grid, even when designing for mobile. This means making positive decisions about spacing and layout—for example, the amount of space we leave between elements and the edge of the screen.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In Figma, it’s super easy to add a column grid. Select the Frame by clicking on it in the Layers panel or on the Canvas, and then hit the “+” icon in the Properties panel under where it says “Layout grid”.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Count: 3 (this creates 3 columns)</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Gutter: 16 (this adds 16 pixels of space between each column)</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Margin: 16 (this adds 16 pixels of space at the edges of the Frame)</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:video -->
<figure class="wp-block-video"><video controls loop muted preload="none" src="https://storage.designlab.com/email-courses/figma-101/2023/F101_D2_02.mp4" playsinline></video></figure>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">3. Add the name of your app</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Let’s create a simple “wordmark” using the Text tool. A wordmark is the name of a company or product set in a beautiful way using text only. (Tomorrow we’ll work on a logo!) Press T to select the Text tool, then click in the frame and type out your app’s name. The settings from when we created the button yesterday may still be in place, so we must make adjustments. Here’s what we’ve set the text to:</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Typeface: Roboto Bold</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Text size: 42 pixels</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Case: lowercase</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">4. Reposition the wordmark</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>If you click and drag on the text you’ve just created, you can move it around the frame to reposition it. If you drag it near the horizontal middle of the frame, you'll notice that guides will pop up to indicate that the text is centered. Once it’s centered, release the mouse button!</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:video -->
<figure class="wp-block-video"><video controls loop muted preload="none" src="https://storage.designlab.com/email-courses/figma-101/2023/F101_D2_04.mp4" playsinline></video></figure>

<p>‍</p>

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">5. Create the username field</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We can easily create some sleek login fields just using rectangles and text. First, select the Rectangle tool&nbsp;R&nbsp;and click and drag to create a rectangle about 250 pixels wide and about 30 pixels high. Notice how Figma displays the size of the shape in numbers as you’re drawing it?</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:video -->
<figure class="wp-block-video"><video controls loop muted preload="none" src="https://storage.designlab.com/email-courses/figma-101/2023/F101_D2_05.mp4" playsinline></video></figure>
<!-- /wp:video -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Change the fill color to be white (#FFFFFF). We then need to change the opacity setting to make the shape semi-transparent. This isn't labeled as Opacity like it is in Adobe Illustrator, but it does exist: it's the number shown in the Properties panel under "Layer" next to where it says, "Pass through." It’s set to 100% by default—let’s change that to 25%. You can also set this by simply selecting the object and typing in the opacity value you want. Try it—select the rectangle and simply type “25”. (This is strangely satisfying, you’ll agree.)</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:video -->
<figure class="wp-block-video"><video controls loop muted preload="none" src="https://storage.designlab.com/email-courses/figma-101/2023/F101_D2_06.mp4" playsinline></video></figure>
<!-- /wp:video -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Next, press&nbsp;T&nbsp;&nbsp;to go back to the Text tool. Click anywhere in the frame, and type “Username”. The font will probably still be set to the preferences you used for the wordmark, so once you’ve finished typing, select the text and change it to Inter Regular, the size to 16 pixels, and the case to “As typed” (the dash). Drag the text label over the rectangle. And presto! A form field.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:video -->
<figure class="wp-block-video"><video controls loop muted preload="none" src="https://storage.designlab.com/email-courses/figma-101/2023/F101_D2_07.mp4" playsinline></video></figure>
<!-- /wp:video -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>As a final step, make sure that the “Username” text is positioned nicely in relation to the rectangle. You can do this by clicking once on the text layer, and then “nudging” it 1 pixel at a time using the arrow keys. If you hold down&nbsp;Shift ⇧&nbsp;while using the arrow keys, the selection will move in 10 pixel increments.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:video -->
<figure class="wp-block-video"><video controls loop muted preload="none" src="https://storage.designlab.com/email-courses/figma-101/2023/F101_D2_08.mp4" playsinline></video></figure>
<!-- /wp:video -->

<p>‍</p>

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">6. Duplicate the username field to create a password field</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>It’s very easy to duplicate elements in Figma. With the Move tool selected&nbsp;V, just drag a marquee (rectangular selection) around the rectangle and text you just created. You can use old-school copy and paste (⌘C&nbsp;then&nbsp;⌘V&nbsp;on Mac, or&nbsp;CtrlC&nbsp;then&nbsp;CtrlV&nbsp;on PC). &nbsp;</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Alternatively, you can hold down&nbsp;Option&nbsp;(Mac) or&nbsp;Alt&nbsp;(PC), and then click and drag from the selection to make a duplicate.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Place your duplicate objects so that they’re sat directly below the username field. Then, simply double-click the “Username” text and change it to “Password”.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:video -->
<figure class="wp-block-video"><video controls loop muted preload="none" src="https://storage.designlab.com/email-courses/figma-101/2023/F101_D2_09.mp4" playsinline></video></figure>
<!-- /wp:video -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>‍</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">7. Duplicate the button you made yesterday.</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Select the “SIGN IN” text and the yellow rectangle with the button you created yesterday. Group them by hitting ⌘G (Mac) or CtrlG (PC). If you look at your Layers panel they’ll now be called “Group 1.” Go ahead and rename that by double-clicking on it in the Layers panel and typing in “Sign In Button.”</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Another way to duplicate elements is by selecting an element on your canvas and hitting ⌘D (Mac) or CtrlD (PC).  Try this with the now grouped button. The duplicate will be placed directly on top of the original—so once you’ve created the copy, just drag it across to the other frame. This shows how easy it is to move elements between frames in Figma—it’s a simple drag and drop.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">8. Make the background more interesting</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In Figma, we can easily apply gradients to the backgrounds of our frames to make them more exciting. Start by selecting the Frame and navigating to the “Fill” section of the Properties panel on the right. Next, click on the blue color swatch right underneath where it says “Fill.” This will open the color panel. At the top of the color panel underneath where it says “Custom,” hit the Gradient button. &nbsp;</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><img src="https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/65130e79c72ae8812db3412e/6580ac53f8254edd997b96e7_F101_D2_11.jpeg" alt="Picking a gradient"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>&nbsp;Still in the color panel, select the right color swatch on the gradient spectrum.&nbsp;</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><img src="https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/65130e79c72ae8812db3412e/6580ac53f8254edd997b96e3_F101_D2_12.jpeg" alt="Adjusting a gradient"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Pick a color that you think transitions nicely into the current background color. Get creative! We’ve gone with a pink (#E36FA6). You might also need to change the opacity like we did.</p>

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">9. Align &amp; refine the layout</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The last thing to do today is to make sure that everything is nicely aligned. Here are some things to check:</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Do the “Username” and “Password” input fields line up with the columns?</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Are all of the interactive elements spaced out enough so that users don’t accidentally tap on the wrong thing?</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Is everything centered to the frame?</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->
