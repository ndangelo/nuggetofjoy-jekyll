<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Understand the common connotations of colors and how they generally affect people's moods</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>There's a reason why healthy ecological products mostly use green packaging. Or why athletes in red uniforms appear more confident and win more often. Or why you become more relaxed and serene among lush green in a park after a long, hard day. Why does it happen, and how does&nbsp;<a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/color" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">color</a>&nbsp;work?</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Color is a powerful tool people use for communicating, stimulating action, influencing mood, and even modifying behavior. It can affect our performance and consumer behavior, increase appetite, cause anxiety, or, oppositely, soothe emotions. However, while some colors evoke universal associations, people may still react differently due to their memories, culture, and current environment.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Understanding different color connotations will help you be more careful while selecting colors for your next design project,&nbsp;<a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/marketing" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">marketing</a>&nbsp;campaign, living room paint colors, or even a new car.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Yellow is commonly linked to feelings of happiness and joy. It is often used to create cheerful and positive environments. It is also known to evoke feelings of:</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Warmth</strong>: Yellow is associated with the warmth of the sun and can create a welcoming and inviting atmosphere.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Optimism</strong>: The bright and light nature of yellow often symbolizes optimism and positivity.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Energy</strong>: Yellow is perceived as energetic and lively. It can stimulate mental activity and generate a sense of excitement.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Attention-grabbing</strong>: Due to its brightness, yellow can quickly catch the eye, which is why it's used in warning signs and advertisements.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>On the flip side, very intense or saturated yellow can be overwhelming and cause visual fatigue. It can also be associated with caution or warning, potentially creating a sense of unease.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In design, avoid using bright yellow for backgrounds as it can cause severe eyestrain and hurt readability. Use it sparingly and only to attract attention to important elements.SaveShare</p>

<!-- wp:image -->

<p>Thinking of an orange fruit often brings to mind its bright, shiny skin and refreshing taste. For many, the color orange is linked to excitement and enthusiasm. <h2 class="wp-block-heading">Sad colors</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/sad-colors-1632307539730/a-1727340008211.jpg" alt="Sad colors"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/sad-colors-1632307539730/a-1727340008211-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The phrase "feeling blue" means feeling sad or depressed. This idea comes from old sailing traditions where blue flags were used when someone died during a voyage.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Artists, like Pablo Picasso, used blue to show sorrow and hardship. During his Blue Period, Picasso painted mostly in blue <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/shades" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">shades</a>, depicting themes of poverty and loneliness. His works, such as <em>The Old Guitarist</em>, are examples of how blue can express deep sadness.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Since blue is a cool color, it can create a calm, still feeling that sometimes feels cold or distant. This can make people feel lonely or sad. Keep in mind that different shades of blue have different connotations. For example, light blue can be calming and serene, while dark blue can feel more somber​.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Gray is another color linked to gloominess and a lack of energy. It can evoke feelings of dullness and depression, especially when used in large amounts.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Pro Tip!</strong> Avoid using pale, low-saturated blue or gray for CTA buttons.SaveShare</p>

<p>Red is often considered a color of winners. At the 2004 Olympic Games in Athens, contestants of 4 combat sports (boxing, tae kwon do, Greco-Roman wrestling, and freestyle wrestling) were randomly assigned to wear a red or blue uniform. <!-- wp:paragraph -->

More importantly, red enhances metabolism and appetite.<sup><a href="https://app.uxcel.com/#anchor-6">[6]</a></sup></p>

<p>Other colors like yellow, orange, bright green, and bright pink also evoke motivation and enthusiasm for life and action.SaveShare</p>

<p>If asked to name the first color that comes to your mind when you hear the word "alarming," you're likely to say "red." Among many cultures, red stands for blood, fire, anger, and even rage. <p>Red is fantastic for warnings and drawing attention but use this color thoughtfully only for critical information to avoid alarming users unnecessarily.</p>
