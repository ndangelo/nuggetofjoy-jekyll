<!-- wp:paragraph {"fontSize":"medium"} -->
<p class="has-medium-font-size">Information architecture principles are guidelines that help designers create organized, user-friendly digital spaces. In 2010, Dan Brown, a&nbsp;<a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/user-experience" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">UX</a>&nbsp;consultant and information architect, introduced eight principles to guide&nbsp;<a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/information-architecture" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">IA</a>&nbsp;thinking. These principles are designed to improve how users interact with information on websites and apps. For example, the principle of choices emphasizes offering users meaningful options. Another key principle is the principle of disclosure, which involves revealing information progressively to avoid overwhelming users. These principles help designers create intuitive, scalable, and user-centered information structures, enhancing the overall user experience.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">The principle of objects</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/the-principle-of-objects-1721052540497/a-1721052540497.jpg" alt="The principle of objects"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/the-principle-of-objects-1721052540497/a-1721052540497-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"fontSize":"medium"} -->
<p class="has-medium-font-size">The principle of objects refers to organizing information into meaningful chunks or units that people can easily understand and interact with. Think of objects as <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/container" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">containers</a> that group related information together, much like how folders on your computer group similar files. For example, let’s say you’re designing a website for a library. You could think of different categories like "Books," "Authors," and "Genres" as objects. Each object contains specific information related to it — like a "Book" object might include the title, author, and publication year, while the "Author" object might have the author’s bio and a list of their books.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"fontSize":"medium"} -->
<p class="has-medium-font-size">By organizing information this way, you help users find what they need more easily. When people visit the library’s website, they don’t have to dig through a pile of random information — instead, they can quickly navigate through well-organized objects that make sense and are easy to explore.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"fontSize":"medium"} -->
<p class="has-medium-font-size">This approach helps make websites, apps, or any information systems more user-friendly, logical, and efficient to navigate.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">The principle of choices</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/the-principle-of-choices-1721053688415/b-1721053688423.jpg" alt="The principle of choices Bad Practice"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/the-principle-of-choices-1721053688415/b-1721053688423-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/the-principle-of-choices-1721053688415/a-1721053688416.jpg" alt="The principle of choices Best Practice"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/the-principle-of-choices-1721053688415/a-1721053688416-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"fontSize":"medium"} -->
<p class="has-medium-font-size">The principle of choices emphasizes the importance of simplicity in user interfaces. Users should never feel overwhelmed by too many options. Instead, presenting a limited number of choices at any given time helps users stay focused and makes <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/navigation" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">navigation</a> easier. For example, an online banking app might limit the main navigation to key actions like checking account balance, transferring funds, and paying bills.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"fontSize":"medium"} -->
<p class="has-medium-font-size">By reducing the number of visible choices, users can accomplish their tasks efficiently without unnecessary distractions. This approach not only improves the user experience but also increases the likelihood of successful <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/interaction" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">interactions</a>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">The principle of disclosure</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/the-principle-of-disclosure-1721053883503/a-1721053883504.jpg" alt="The principle of disclosure"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/the-principle-of-disclosure-1721053883503/a-1721053883504-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"fontSize":"medium"} -->
<p class="has-medium-font-size">The principle of disclosure emphasizes revealing only relevant information as needed. By showing users just what they need to complete their task, you prevent them from feeling overwhelmed and help them make informed decisions on how to proceed. For example, an online shopping site might first show a product's key features and <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/price" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">price</a>. If users are interested, they can click to see more details like specifications, customer reviews, and related products. This approach keeps the initial interface clean and simple, helping users focus on what matters most at each stage of their journey.SaveShare</p>

<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/the-principle-of-exemplars-1721053955208/a-1721053955208.jpg" alt="The principle of exemplars Best Practice"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/the-principle-of-exemplars-1721053955208/a-1721053955208-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"fontSize":"medium"} -->
<p class="has-medium-font-size">The principle of exemplars highlights the use of specific examples to illustrate how elements work together, rather than relying solely on descriptions. This approach helps users quickly understand and navigate an interface through a more intuitive, visual language. For instance, a <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/project-management" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">project management</a> tool could use <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/icons" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">icons</a> to represent different task statuses — like a green checkmark for completed tasks, a yellow clock for tasks in progress, and a red exclamation mark for urgent tasks. These icons help users quickly grasp the status of their projects at a glance, without needing to read through detailed descriptions.SaveShare</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">The principle of front doors</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/the-principle-of-front-doors-1721054297548/b-1721054297561.jpg" alt="The principle of front doors Bad Practice"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/the-principle-of-front-doors-1721054297548/b-1721054297561-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/the-principle-of-front-doors-1721054297548/a-1721054297548.jpg" alt="The principle of front doors Best Practice"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/the-principle-of-front-doors-1721054297548/a-1721054297548-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"fontSize":"medium"} -->
<p class="has-medium-font-size">The principle of front doors emphasizes that not all users will start their journey on your homepage. Regardless of which <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/pages" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">page</a> they land on, users should be able to find what they need quickly and easily. This means every page should serve as a helpful entry point, guiding users to the main areas of your site. For example, consider an e-commerce website. A user might land directly on a product page from a <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/search" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">search</a> engine. This page should provide clear navigation options to other parts of the site, such as categories, related products, and <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/support" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">customer support</a>. This way, even if the product they originally searched for isn't suitable, they can still easily explore and find alternatives.SaveShare</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">The principle of multiple classification</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/the-principle-of-multiple-classification-1721055899193/b-1721055899210.jpg" alt="The principle of multiple classification Bad Practice"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/the-principle-of-multiple-classification-1721055899193/b-1721055899210-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/the-principle-of-multiple-classification-1721055899193/a-1721055899193.jpg" alt="The principle of multiple classification Best Practice"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/the-principle-of-multiple-classification-1721055899193/a-1721055899193-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"fontSize":"medium"} -->
<p class="has-medium-font-size">The principle of multiple classification acknowledges that people navigate interfaces differently. It's the responsibility of the information architect to provide multiple classification systems tailored to diverse needs, ensuring everyone can find what they’re looking for efficiently. For example, consider an online clothing store. Some users might prefer browsing by category, such as "Men," "Women," and "Kids," while others might search for specific items like "jeans" or "jackets." To accommodate these preferences, the store should offer a <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/menus" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">menu</a> bar with broad categories and a search bar for direct queries. Additionally, <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/filters" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">filters</a> like size, <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/color" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">color</a>, and <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/branding" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">brand</a> can help users narrow down their options further.SaveShare</p>

<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/the-principle-of-navigation-1721055960245/b-1721807546419.jpg" alt="The principle of navigation Bad Practice"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/the-principle-of-navigation-1721055960245/b-1721807546419-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/the-principle-of-navigation-1721055960245/a-1721807546417.jpg" alt="The principle of navigation Best Practice"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/the-principle-of-navigation-1721055960245/a-1721807546417-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"fontSize":"medium"} -->
<p class="has-medium-font-size">The principle of navigation is about ensuring users can move through your site or app easily and intuitively. Clear, consistent navigation helps users quickly find what they’re looking for, whether they're starting on the homepage or <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/landing-page" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">landing</a> on a specific page from a search engine. For example, imagine a bookstore app. To create effective navigation, you need to understand users' needs and their way of browsing a book catalog. The main navigation menu might include categories like "Fiction," "Non-Fiction," and "Kids &amp; Teens." Each category should have subcategories based on common genres, for example, "Thrillers" under Fiction and "Business Books" under “Non-Fiction.”SaveShare</p>

<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/the-principle-of-growth-1721056060224/b-1721056060227.jpg" alt="The principle of growth Bad Practice"/></figure>

<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/the-principle-of-growth-1721056060224/a-1721056060224.jpg" alt="The principle of growth Best Practice"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/the-principle-of-growth-1721056060224/a-1721056060224-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"fontSize":"medium"} -->
<p class="has-medium-font-size">The principle of growth emphasizes designing systems that can evolve over time. As more information is added, the interface should maintain its <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/usability" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">usability</a> and clarity, ensuring it doesn't become cluttered or confusing. Imagine, for example, a corporate intranet designed to store company documents and resources. At launch, it might only include basic HR policies and forms. Over time, additional documents like training materials, project archives, and departmental guidelines will be added.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"fontSize":"medium"} -->
<p class="has-medium-font-size">A well-thought-out IA will have a scalable folder structure and search functionality, allowing new <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/content" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">content</a> to be organized logically without overwhelming users. By anticipating future growth, you can create information architectures that remain efficient and user-friendly, even as content and user needs evolve.</p>
