<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="toc-9myq-how-to-create-frosted-glass-effect-in-photoshop">1.&nbsp;How to Create a Frosted Glass Effect in Photoshop</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Step 1</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Let's start with the most basic Photoshop glass effect. Open your image in Photoshop,<strong> right-click </strong>it, and select <strong>Convert to Smart Object</strong>. This way, you can easily replace the photo later, while keeping the glass effect intact!</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><img src="https://cms-assets.tutsplus.com/cdn-cgi/image/width=850/uploads/users/108/posts/93789/image-upload/glass_effect_photoshop_1_1.png" alt="turn layer into smart object"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="toc-1yjl-step">Step 2</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Duplicate&nbsp;</strong>the Smart Object<strong>&nbsp;(Control-J)&nbsp;</strong>and put it into a group<strong>&nbsp;(Control-G)</strong>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><img src="https://cms-assets.tutsplus.com/cdn-cgi/image/width=850/uploads/users/108/posts/93789/image-upload/glass_effect_photoshop_1_2.png" alt="put layer into group"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="toc-uj3i-step">Step 3</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Use the&nbsp;<strong>Rectangular Marquee Tool (M)</strong>, or any other selection tool, to select the area that you want to apply your frosted glass effect to. Then add a<strong>&nbsp;Layer Mask</strong>&nbsp;to the group.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center","linkDestination":"custom"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><img src="https://cms-assets.tutsplus.com/cdn-cgi/image/width=850/uploads/users/108/posts/93789/image-upload/glass_effect_photoshop_1_4.jpg" alt="make a selection"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><img src="https://cms-assets.tutsplus.com/cdn-cgi/image/width=850/uploads/users/108/posts/93789/image-upload/glass_effect_photoshop_1_3.png" alt="add a layer mask to the group"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="toc-w6lg-step">Step 4</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Select the layer inside the group and go to <strong>Filter > Blur > Gaussian Blur</strong>. Adjust the <strong>Radius </strong>to achieve the desired effect.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><img src="https://cms-assets.tutsplus.com/cdn-cgi/image/width=850/uploads/users/108/posts/93789/image-upload/glass_effect_photoshop_1_5.jpg" alt="add gaussian blur"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="toc-1ygg-step">Step 5</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Our Photoshop frosted glass effect is almost done, but remember that a matte glass effect doesn't only blur the image—it also brightens it! So let's brighten the image by using a<strong>&nbsp;Hue/Saturation</strong>&nbsp;Adjustment Layer. Increase the&nbsp;<strong>Lightness&nbsp;</strong>to about&nbsp;<strong>30</strong>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><img src="https://cms-assets.tutsplus.com/cdn-cgi/image/width=850/uploads/users/108/posts/93789/image-upload/glass_effect_photoshop_1_6.png" alt="add hue saturation"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><img src="https://cms-assets.tutsplus.com/cdn-cgi/image/width=850/uploads/users/108/posts/93789/image-upload/glass_effect_photoshop_1_7.png" alt="hue saturation adjustments"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><img src="https://cms-assets.tutsplus.com/cdn-cgi/image/width=850/uploads/users/108/posts/93789/image-upload/glass_effect_photoshop_1_8.jpg" alt="brightened image"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="toc-h8v1-step">Step 6</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>To make it more convincing, change the&nbsp;<strong>Blend Mode&nbsp;</strong>to&nbsp;<strong>Lighter Color</strong>, and adjust the&nbsp;<strong>Opacity</strong>&nbsp;to about&nbsp;<strong>50%</strong>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><img src="https://cms-assets.tutsplus.com/cdn-cgi/image/width=850/uploads/users/108/posts/93789/image-upload/glass_effect_photoshop_1_9.png" alt="change blend mode to lighter color"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>And that's it: your Photoshop frosted glass effect is done!</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><img src="https://cms-assets.tutsplus.com/cdn-cgi/image/width=850/uploads/users/108/posts/93789/image-upload/glass_effect_photoshop_final_3.jpg" alt="fina frosted glass effect"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="toc-kxuf-how-to-create-textured-frosted-glass-effect-in-photoshop">2.&nbsp;How to Create a Textured Frosted Glass Effect in Photoshop</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="toc-fykr-step">Step 1</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>If you want to create a more detailed glass texture in Photoshop, you can start in the same way: turn your image into a Smart Object&nbsp;<strong>(right-click &gt; Convert to Smart Object)</strong>.</p>

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><img src="https://cms-assets.tutsplus.com/cdn-cgi/image/width=850/uploads/users/108/posts/93789/image-upload/glass_effect_photoshop_2_1.png" alt="turn layer into smart object"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="toc-7jj4-step">Step 2</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Duplicate&nbsp;</strong>the Smart Object<strong>&nbsp;(Control-J)</strong>&nbsp;and put it into a group<strong>&nbsp;(Control-G)</strong>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><img src="https://cms-assets.tutsplus.com/cdn-cgi/image/width=850/uploads/users/108/posts/93789/image-upload/glass_effect_photoshop_2_2.png" alt="place the layer into group"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="toc-pfco-step">Step 3</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Use the&nbsp;<strong>Rectangular Marquee Tool (M)</strong>, or any other selection tool, to select the area that you want to apply your textured glass effect to. Then add a&nbsp;<strong>Layer Mask&nbsp;</strong>to the group.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center","linkDestination":"custom"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><img src="https://cms-assets.tutsplus.com/cdn-cgi/image/width=850/uploads/users/108/posts/93789/image-upload/glass_effect_photoshop_2_4.jpg" alt="select the area"/></figure>

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center"} -->

<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="toc-loor-step">Step 4</h3>

Open the&nbsp;<strong>Distort&nbsp;</strong>tab and select&nbsp;<strong>Glass</strong>. Use the&nbsp;<strong>Frosted&nbsp;</strong>texture. Adjust the settings to create the effect you need—the exact values will depend on the size of the photo (mine is 2000 px wide), as well as your own vision. Here are mine:</p>

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Distortion</strong>: 19</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Smoothness</strong>: 3</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Scaling</strong>: 139%</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><img src="https://cms-assets.tutsplus.com/cdn-cgi/image/width=850/uploads/users/108/posts/93789/image-upload/glass_effect_photoshop_2_5.jpg" alt="add glass filter"/></figure>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="toc-clqo-step">Step 5</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The textured frosted glass effect is visible now, but it looks a bit rough. Let's make a couple of adjustments. <strong>Duplicate </strong>this layer <strong>(Control-J)</strong> and <strong>double-click</strong> its <strong>Filter Gallery</strong> filter. Change the values to make the frosting smaller, almost like noise. Here are my values:</p>

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><img src="https://cms-assets.tutsplus.com/cdn-cgi/image/width=850/uploads/users/108/posts/93789/image-upload/glass_effect_photoshop_2_9.jpg" alt="add gaussian blur"/></figure>
