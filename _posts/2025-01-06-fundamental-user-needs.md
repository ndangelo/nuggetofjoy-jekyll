<p>Learn how to integrate fundamental psychological needs in design to enhance user loyalty and engagement</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/lessons/fundamental-user-needs-1718006528581-2x.svg" alt="Fundamental User Needs Lesson"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Self-determination theory (SDT), developed by psychologists Richard Ryan and Edward Deci, highlights 3 basic psychological needs that drive human motivation: autonomy, relatedness, and competence. These needs are essential for guiding how we use our time and energy efficiently, as they deeply influence our motivation to engage in activities.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Understanding these 3 psychological needs — autonomy, relatedness, and competence — is crucial for&nbsp;<a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/ux-design" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">UX design</a>&nbsp;because they fundamentally shape user engagement and satisfaction. When users feel a sense of control, connection, and proficiency in their&nbsp;<a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/interaction" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">interactions</a>&nbsp;with digital platforms, they are more motivated and likely to have a positive experience. Effectively meeting these needs in design not only enhances&nbsp;<a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/usability" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">usability</a>&nbsp;but also deepens users' emotional connection to the product, encouraging longer and more meaningful engagement.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Autonomy</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/autonomy-1716563640175/a-1716563640175.jpg" alt="Autonomy"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/autonomy-1716563640175/a-1716563640175-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Self-determination theory (SDT), created by Edward L. Deci and Richard M. Ryan in the 1980s, is a psychological framework focused on understanding what drives human motivation. It identifies 3 key psychological needs, with autonomy being one of them. Autonomy is the desire for <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/independence" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">independence</a> and the ability to make choices that align with our personal values and goals. This need greatly influences motivation and well-being.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Consider the difference between choosing a hobby and being forced to do a chore. When we pursue a hobby, we're following our interests and feel a sense of control and satisfaction. Conversely, mandatory chores feel frustrating or restrictive because we're given no choice. By understanding and encouraging autonomy, designers can create environments where people feel more motivated, valued, and empowered.SaveShare</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Relatedness</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/relatedness-1716563681795/a-1716563681795.jpg" alt="Relatedness"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/relatedness-1716563681795/a-1716563681795-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The second need in SDT is relatedness, which refers to the human need to feel understood and supported by others. We are social creatures who thrive on meaningful connections, whether we're introverted or extroverted.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Consider an individual who has emigrated to a new country. Initially, they may feel isolated and stressed about fitting into a new culture. However, by joining community events, language-learning clubs, or even attending a local yoga studio, they can gradually build relationships that make them feel supported and understood. These connections help fulfill their need for relatedness, providing them with a sense of belonging in a new environment.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In design, fostering relatedness means understanding and supporting users throughout their journey. An online platform that encourages collaboration or shared interests can create a sense of community. User <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/interview" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">interviews</a> and diary studies can reveal insights that designers can use to build products that resonate with the need for relatedness.SaveShare</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Competence</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/competence-1716563723309/a-1716563723309.jpg" alt="Competence"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/competence-1716563723309/a-1716563723309-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Competence is about efficiently succeeding in tasks and feeling confident in our abilities. For instance, consider someone learning to play guitar for the first time. Initially, they struggle with finger placement, hit the wrong notes, and may feel discouraged when they compare themselves to more skilled guitarists. If asked to play again, they might hesitate due to feeling incompetent. But with time, practice, and guidance, they can develop skills and eventually play with ease and confidence.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>As designers, we're eager to pack our products with helpful features and services. However, these features can be neglected if we don't help users become proficient with them. Promoting competence in system use leads to greater satisfaction, safety, and <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/design-metrics" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">efficiency</a>.<sup><a href="https://app.uxcel.com/#anchor-1">[1]</a></sup>SaveShare</p>

<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/allow-users-to-customize-content-1716563771614/a-1716563771614.jpg" alt="Allow users to customize content Best Practice"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/allow-users-to-customize-content-1716563771614/a-1716563771614-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Allowing users to customize <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/content" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">content</a> within an interface is a key method for providing them with autonomy. This ability fosters a sense of ownership and personal control over their experience. While not every user spends time personalizing <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/settings" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">settings</a>, simple customizations can significantly enhance their satisfaction. A practical example of this is on streaming platforms, where users can create personalized playlists based on their preferences. These playlists don't change the core features of the platform, but they allow users to curate and access their favorite media easily, making the platform feel like their own space.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Customization enables users to tailor their experience without compromising usability and consistency. It gives them control over how they interact with the platform while still aligning with familiar, standardized structures.SaveShare</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Provide users with a choice</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/provide-users-with-a-choice-1716563835977/b-1716563835978.jpg" alt="Provide users with a choice Bad Practice"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/provide-users-with-a-choice-1716563835977/b-1716563835978-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/provide-users-with-a-choice-1716563835977/a-1716563835977.jpg" alt="Provide users with a choice Best Practice"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/provide-users-with-a-choice-1716563835977/a-1716563835977-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"fontSize":"medium"} -->
<p class="has-medium-font-size">Providing user autonomy by offering a suitable number of choices is a balancing act. Users feel empowered when they have control over their experience, but too many options can lead to decision paralysis.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"fontSize":"medium"} -->
<p class="has-medium-font-size">Rather than overwhelming users with too many settings or paths, focus on providing a limited selection of meaningful choices. A streaming platform that allows users to filter shows and movies by only a handful of key criteria, such as genre, release year, or language, is a safer choice than offering a variety of niche <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/filters" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">filters</a>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"fontSize":"medium"} -->
<p class="has-medium-font-size">This simple approach to filtering provides enough flexibility for users to personalize their viewing experience without bombarding them with complex or redundant options.<sup><a href="https://app.uxcel.com/#anchor-2">[2]</a></sup>SaveShare</p>

<!-- wp:paragraph {"fontSize":"medium"} -->
<p class="has-medium-font-size">Providing scannability in <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/pages" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">page</a> <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/layout-composition" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">layouts</a> and content is a crucial method for granting users autonomy. <!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/offer-multiple-interactive-options-1716563963402/a-1716563963402.jpg" alt="Offer multiple interactive options Best Practice"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/offer-multiple-interactive-options-1716563963402/a-1716563963402-2x.jpg" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Allowing users to choose when and in what order they interact with content significantly enhances their sense of autonomy. This flexibility acknowledges that users have different needs and levels of familiarity with a platform. For instance, someone well-acquainted with a website might skip introductory content, while a new visitor might prefer a guided tour.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>YouTube effectively provides autonomy by letting viewers select which videos to watch and in what order. Users can jump directly to videos of interest or explore suggested playlists based on their viewing history. This personalized approach accommodates users at different familiarity levels and with various interests, enhancing their control and satisfaction.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Conversely, requiring users to enter personal information before accessing basic services can feel forced and frustrating. By allowing users to skip steps, choose content paths, and offering them multiple interaction methods, designers can create more engaging, user-friendly, and empowering environments.SaveShare</p>

<p class="has-medium-font-size">Fulfilling users' need for competence involves providing timely help to ensure they can quickly and efficiently master a system. <p class="has-medium-font-size">Minimizing the learning curve of a system is essential to fulfilling users' need for competence. <h2 class="wp-block-heading">Determine the priority of needs</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/determine-the-priority-of-needs-1716564695185/a-1718006040008-2x.mp4" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a><a target="_blank" href="https://img.uxcel.com/practices/determine-the-priority-of-needs-1716564695185/a-1718006040008-2x.mp4" rel="noreferrer noopener"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"fontSize":"medium"} -->
<p class="has-medium-font-size">When designing interfaces that need to meet conflicting user needs like autonomy, relatedness, and competence, finding the right balance is crucial. For example, consider a tax filing software with a guided setup. This wizard helps users <a href="https://app.uxcel.com/glossary/inputs" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">input</a> complex data correctly, enhancing their competence and confidence. However, this structured guidance also limits their freedom to navigate the tool independently, reducing autonomy.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"fontSize":"medium"} -->
<p class="has-medium-font-size">In such scenarios, it's important to test the design with real users to see what they value more. If users prefer the ease and accuracy that comes with guided steps, focusing on competence may be the right choice. On the other hand, if they value the freedom to explore and make choices, even at the cost of facing more complex interactions, then autonomy should be prioritized.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
