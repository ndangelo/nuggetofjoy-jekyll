<h2><strong>We are taking a look at how the Type on a Path&nbsp;Tool can&nbsp;give your typography a curved, fluid style that looks fantastic layered over photography.</strong></h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In this series of tutorials,<strong>&nbsp;<a href="https://indesignskills.com/tag/how-did-they-do-that-tutorial/">How Did They Do That?</a></strong>, we dissect the tricks used by graphic design pros and share surprisingly simple tips that will make your InDesign work&nbsp;look ultra-professional, even if you’re a beginner to the software!</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:separator -->
<hr class="wp-block-separator has-alpha-channel-opacity"/>
<!-- /wp:separator -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3><strong>Examples of the Type on a Path Tool</strong></h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:separator -->
<hr class="wp-block-separator has-alpha-channel-opacity"/>
<!-- /wp:separator -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We’re going to focus on how the&nbsp;<strong>Type on a Path Tool</strong>&nbsp;can be&nbsp;used in magazine&nbsp;design.&nbsp;Image + typography might be the consistent formula for selling issues, but this doesn’t mean the formula needs to be conventional. Magazine designers&nbsp;are always on the lookout&nbsp;for new ways to reinvent their covers.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Layering text in interesting, fluid ways over the top of striking photography is an attention-grabbing way to make covers look more artistic and exciting. It’s also a great technique for making&nbsp;the text&nbsp;interact more naturally with the curves, lines and shapes of an image.</p>

<p>This technique might look tricky, but it’s anything but. You can recreate the style easily in Adobe InDesign with the&nbsp;<strong>Type on a Path</strong>&nbsp;and&nbsp;<strong>Pen&nbsp;Tools</strong>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>It all comes down to a few simple steps…</p>

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3><strong>Step 1:&nbsp;Create a New Layer for Your Typography</strong></h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:separator -->
<hr class="wp-block-separator has-alpha-channel-opacity"/>
<!-- /wp:separator -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Create a basic cover in Adobe InDesign</strong>, to a size that you prefer. On the existing layer,<strong>&nbsp;use the Rectangle Frame Tool (F)</strong>&nbsp;to create a new image frame, and&nbsp;<strong>File &gt; Place a photo in the frame</strong>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->

<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->

<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Look for photos with strong shapes and curves—portraits of people work well, as do aerial photos with plenty of white space in-between items on the photo (this doesn’t have to be actually white, just one plain background color which will allow text to appear legible).</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center","id":5780,"linkDestination":"custom"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><a href="https://i0.wp.com/indesignskills.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/01/1.jpg?ssl=1"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/1-5.jpg" alt="indesign curved text type on a path organic fluid typography magazine cover photo" class="wp-image-5780"/></a></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>I’ve used this aerial shot of rolls of bread on a wooden board. There’s plenty of curves and lots of space for placing text, without being interrupted with too much detail beneath.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Create a new layer from the Layers panel</strong>&nbsp;(choose New Layer from the panel’s drop-down menu), and&nbsp;<strong>rename the layer Typography.</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center","id":5777,"linkDestination":"custom"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><a href="https://i0.wp.com/indesignskills.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/01/1.1.png?ssl=1"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/1.1-2.png" alt="indesign curved text type on a path organic fluid typography layers panel" class="wp-image-5777"/></a></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Lock the bottom layer</strong>, to avoid moving the photo around by mistake. You can make room for a header, and subtitle at the top of the page.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center","id":5778,"linkDestination":"custom"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><a href="https://i0.wp.com/indesignskills.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/01/1.2.jpg?ssl=1"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/1.2-5.jpg" alt="indesign curved text type on a path organic fluid typography magazine cover" class="wp-image-5778"/></a></figure>

<!-- /wp:separator -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3><strong>Step 2:&nbsp;Use the Pen Tool (P)</strong></h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:separator -->
<hr class="wp-block-separator has-alpha-channel-opacity"/>
<!-- /wp:separator -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>From the&nbsp;<strong>Tools panel</strong>&nbsp;choose the&nbsp;<strong>Pen Tool</strong>, or&nbsp;<strong>hit P on the keyboard.</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center","id":5784,"linkDestination":"custom"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><a href="https://i0.wp.com/indesignskills.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/01/Pen-tool.png?ssl=1"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/Pen-tool.png" alt="indesign curved text type on a path organic fluid typography pen tool" class="wp-image-5784"/></a></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>You can use the&nbsp;<strong>Pen Tool</strong>&nbsp;in two ways. One, by simply clicking onto the page and creating a series of joined straight lines. The second way, which we’re going to use here, is to click and drag onto the page to create a curved line, then click again to create a second curve joined to the first and so on</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Move across to the pasteboard, next to the page, to experiment with creating curved lines with the&nbsp;<strong>Pen Tool</strong>. You can do this quite easily without a graphics tablet, but it might take a little bit of practice to get the curves looking really smooth and fluid.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center","id":5783,"linkDestination":"custom"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><a href="https://i0.wp.com/indesignskills.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/01/pen-tool-pasteboard.png?ssl=1"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/pen-tool-pasteboard.png" alt="indesign curved text type on a path organic fluid typography pen tool" class="wp-image-5783"/></a></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Move back onto the page, and&nbsp;<strong>use the Pen Tool&nbsp;to draw smooth curves</strong>&nbsp;around one section of your selected photo.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>You can use the<strong>&nbsp;Direct Selection Tool (A)&nbsp;</strong>to select the anchor points and handlesof the line, and adjust them by either dragging or using the arrows on the keyboard. You may also find it useful to set the<strong>&nbsp;Stroke Color&nbsp;</strong>of the line to a bright, contrasting color for now, to make it easier to see.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center","id":5779,"linkDestination":"custom"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><a href="https://i0.wp.com/indesignskills.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/01/1.3.jpg?ssl=1"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/1.3-1.jpg" alt="indesign curved text type on a path organic fluid typography pen tool" class="wp-image-5779"/></a></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:separator -->
<hr class="wp-block-separator has-alpha-channel-opacity"/>
<!-- /wp:separator -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3><strong>Step 3:&nbsp;Use the&nbsp;Type on a Path Tool (Shift+T)</strong></h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:separator -->
<hr class="wp-block-separator has-alpha-channel-opacity"/>
<!-- /wp:separator -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Once you’ve created your line, head back over to the&nbsp;<strong>Tools&nbsp;</strong>panel. From the<strong>&nbsp;Type Tool drop-down menu</strong>, choose the<strong>&nbsp;Type on a Path Tool (Shift+T).</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Hover your cursor over one edge of the line and click once. The curved line will be transformed into a type path. You can now type in text, and apply formatting in the same way as you would for text in a text frame.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Remove the color of the<strong>&nbsp;Stroke</strong>&nbsp;and set it to<strong>&nbsp;[None]</strong>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Here, I’ve used two fonts to create a bold, uppercase effect, set in a white, [Paper], color—<a href="https://www.myfonts.com/fonts/itc/aachen/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">Aachen Std</a>…</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center","id":5782,"linkDestination":"custom"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><a href="https://i0.wp.com/indesignskills.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/01/curved-type-bread.jpg?ssl=1"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/curved-type-bread.jpg" alt="indesign curved text type on a path organic fluid typography type on a path tool" class="wp-image-5782"/></a></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>…and<strong>&nbsp;<a href="https://www.dafont.com/return-to-sender.font" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">Return to Sender</a><a href="https://indesignskills.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/01/curved-type-aachen.jpg">.</a></strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center","id":5781,"linkDestination":"custom"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><a href="https://i0.wp.com/indesignskills.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/01/curved-type-aachen.jpg?ssl=1"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/curved-type-aachen.jpg" alt="indesign curved text type on a path organic fluid typography type on a path tool" class="wp-image-5781"/></a></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Repeat the process, using the&nbsp;<strong>Pen Tool (P)</strong>&nbsp;to draw curved lines around parts of the photo, and then transform them into text paths using the&nbsp;<strong>Type on a Path Tool (Shift+T).</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"align":"center","id":5788,"linkDestination":"custom"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter"><a href="https://i0.wp.com/indesignskills.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/01/curved-lines-sample.jpg?ssl=1"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/curved-lines-sample.jpg" alt="indesign curved text type on a path organic fluid typography type on a path tool" class="wp-image-5788"/></a></figure>

<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Format the text using the&nbsp;<strong>Formatting Controls panel</strong>&nbsp;at the top of the workspace, or from the&nbsp;<strong>Character panel</strong>&nbsp;(<strong>Window &gt; Type &amp; Tables &gt; Character</strong>).<strong>Vary the Font Size, Font and Color</strong>&nbsp;to create an eclectic, interesting look.</p>
