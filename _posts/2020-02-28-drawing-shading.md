<h2>What is shading in art?</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>With visual arts—especially pencil drawing—shading is very much necessary to bring depth to your work, adding dimension and defining where your subject is in relation to your light source.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>It starts with&nbsp;<a href="https://thevirtualinstructor.com/Value.html" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">value</a>, which is the lightness or darkness of a color.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Shading refers to the darker values that, when combined with tints (lighter values), produce a more realistic masterpiece.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>However, there are many elements of shading and techniques to learn first!</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2>Elements of shading:</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3>Cast shadow</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The cast shadow is the darkest point on your drawing. It falls opposite the light source and represents where the object prevents the light from penetrating.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3>Shadow edge</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>This is where the object is turning away from you and is lighter than the cast shadow.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3>Halftone</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>This is the mid-gray of the object. It’s neither in direct light or shadow.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3>Reflected light</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The reflected light is a light-gray tone. It’s found along the edge of an object and separates the cast shadow and shadow edge.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3>Full light</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>This area is stark white and shows where the light source is directly hitting the object.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>When you understand how shading can affect how we perceive light to be drawn, your artworks will reach a stunning realism.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2>Types of shading techniques</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3>Blending &amp; Rendering</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>These are actually two techniques that work together to create our smooth finished product.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Blending uses different amounts of pressure to determine the values on your subject.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Use the medium itself or a blending tool to help you.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Then you can render the image to remove some of the density to create lighter areas. It’s a back-and-forth type of technique!</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3>Hatching</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>There are <a rel="noreferrer noopener" href="https://www.mybluprint.com/article/this-hatching-exercise-will-make-your-drawings-better" target="_blank">several types of hatching</a> that exist.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>This technique is when you simply let your lines travel together to create dimension.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Every time you put that pen or pencil to paper, keep the lines going in the same direction. The closer your lines get, the darker that area of the image will be.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3>Cross-hatching</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Using the same lines as we did with hatching, cross-hatching is exactly what the name suggests: cross the lines.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The higher the density at which you are crossing the lines will determine the darker and lighter values.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3>Stippling</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>With stippling, your goal is to express the gradation of value with dots, dots, and more dots.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>And just like with the other shading techniques, the density of your dots will dictate the value throughout your subject matter.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
