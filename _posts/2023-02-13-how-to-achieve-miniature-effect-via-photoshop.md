<p>Tilt-shift photography refers to the use of camera movements on small and medium format cameras and sometimes specifically refers to tilt for selective focus, often for simulating a miniature scene. Sometimes, the term is used when the shallow depth of field is simulated with digital post-processing; the name may derive from the tilt-shift lens normally required when the effect is produced optically. (Wiki)<br><br>Since I don't have the expensive tilt-shift lens and I don't have enough time to make my lens for the miniature effect, I experimented on some stock photos I have via Photoshop CS4 instead. It's quite easy, and it brings you the exact effect you want. Miniature effect has been a popular medium used in commercials lately. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Step 1: Choosing the Right Photo</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image {"linkDestination":"custom"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><a href="https://content.instructables.com/F34/K4JT/GU1I4GG6/F34K4JTGU1I4GG6.jpg?auto=webp&amp;frame=1&amp;width=1024&amp;fit=bounds&amp;md=fd53abb38d332cbc127473c0f18278d7"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/F34K4JTGU1I4GG6.jpg" alt="Choosing the Right Photo"/></a></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><br><strong>Not all photos can give the right miniature effect. When choosing photos one must remember the following:</strong><br><br>1) Choose a picture that was taken from afar.<br>2) It is also advisable to choose an image not only that it was taken from afar but rather on an elevated level, like you're on the 4th or 5th floor of a building, taking city streets down below. A good perspective is always a key point.<br>3) Most of the time, cars, buildings, and trees are the best subjects, although if you want to take pictures of people, make sure that there's an element in the picture that would make them look like plastic toy models, for example a building or at least a few cars, trees any structure.<br><br>For this tutorial, I chose one of my favorite miniature effect subjects, trains. Take note that I chose a photo that was taken a floor higher than the subject.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Step 2: Getting Started</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image {"linkDestination":"custom"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><a href="https://content.instructables.com/FED/WCWM/GU1I4GGG/FEDWCWMGU1I4GGG.jpg?auto=webp&amp;frame=1&amp;width=1024&amp;fit=bounds&amp;md=b64ca3a86b5d9e276fbb0b4b5d5e3dcd"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/FEDWCWMGU1I4GGG.jpg" alt="Getting Started"/></a></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><br>Open up your chosen image via Photoshop. In my case, I used CS4.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Step 3:</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/FCS1XR2GU2QYQPS.jpg" alt=""/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a href="https://content.instructables.com/FCS/1XR2/GU2QYQPS/FCS1XR2GU2QYQPS.jpg?auto=webp&amp;frame=1&amp;width=1024&amp;fit=bounds&amp;md=4c4ddc722616319b023a83aa60f75078"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After opening the image to your Photoshop press on "<strong>Q</strong>" to enable Quick Mask </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Step 4:</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/FDK7C0UGU1I4GJD-scaled.jpg" alt=""/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a href="https://content.instructables.com/FDK/7C0U/GU1I4GJD/FDK7C0UGU1I4GJD.jpg?auto=webp&amp;frame=1&amp;width=1024&amp;height=1024&amp;fit=bounds&amp;md=8267768532d37203190a55acb6b8b3f7"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><br>After pressing "<strong>Q</strong>" you may then press "<strong>G</strong>" to enable Gradient. Choose the Reflected Gradient for the miniature effect.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Step 5:</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/FVI49QIGU1UZ142.jpg" alt=""/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a href="https://content.instructables.com/FVI/49QI/GU1UZ142/FVI49QIGU1UZ142.jpg?auto=webp&amp;frame=1&amp;width=1024&amp;fit=bounds&amp;md=09161eb5b520ac85e1ec95e3ca1bc4d1"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><br>After enabling "Gradient," draw a vertical line on the picture. After drawing a vertical line, a reddish color gradient should appear, which will mark that those parts of the image will be the focus or the subject of your picture and not be blurred.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Step 6:</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/F8H0CM4GU1UZ147.jpg" alt=""/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a href="https://content.instructables.com/F8H/0CM4/GU1UZ147/F8H0CM4GU1UZ147.jpg?auto=webp&amp;frame=1&amp;width=1024&amp;fit=bounds&amp;md=09161eb5b520ac85e1ec95e3ca1bc4d1"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><br>After determining the focus and subject of your picture, press on "<strong>Q</strong>" again to exit Quick Mask Mode and go back to Standard Mode. Clicking on this will make the marching ants selection lines appear where our "Lens Blur" should be applied later.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Step 7:</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/FORCXIEGU1I4GMR.jpg" alt=""/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a href="https://content.instructables.com/FOR/CXIE/GU1I4GMR/FORCXIEGU1I4GMR.jpg?auto=webp&amp;frame=1&amp;width=1024&amp;fit=bounds&amp;md=09161eb5b520ac85e1ec95e3ca1bc4d1"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><br>After exiting the Quick Mode, go to&nbsp;<strong>Filter &gt; Blur &gt; Lens Blur</strong>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Step 8:</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/FON0CQDGU2F3OKO.jpg" alt=""/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a href="https://content.instructables.com/FON/0CQD/GU2F3OKO/FON0CQDGU2F3OKO.jpg?auto=webp&amp;frame=1&amp;width=1024&amp;fit=bounds&amp;md=09161eb5b520ac85e1ec95e3ca1bc4d1"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><br>Adjust the radius until you reach the desired output, and press "OK." In my case, I adjusted the radius until 40.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Step 9:</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image {"linkDestination":"custom"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><a href="https://content.instructables.com/FHT/5RUU/GU1I4GO7/FHT5RUUGU1I4GO7.jpg?auto=webp&amp;frame=1&amp;width=1024&amp;fit=bounds&amp;md=09161eb5b520ac85e1ec95e3ca1bc4d1"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/FHT5RUUGU1I4GO7.jpg" alt=""/></a></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Press CTRL-D on the keyboard to remove the marching ants selection boundary.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Step 10:</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/FU6QTO7GU0L7P3I.jpg" alt=""/></figure>

<p><a href="https://content.instructables.com/FBJ/HNVW/GU1I4GOI/FBJHNVWGU1I4GOI.jpg?auto=webp&amp;frame=1&amp;width=1024&amp;fit=bounds&amp;md=2d37b24448627fb31cdc419f5b900c6f"></a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><br>Once done with the focus, you need to enhance the saturation of your image. To achieve the miniature effect, most of the time, the saturation of the images is adjusted to 40 above the normal saturation since miniature models are usually brightly painted. So to achieve the desired output, you need to adjust either the vibrance or the saturation of the image. Just go to<strong> Image > Adjustments > Hue/Saturation</strong>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
